## Q1. What inductive bias does each family encode, and when does it win or lose?

### Takeaway
Each family is a different bet about structure: MLP = none (or token-independence), CNN = locality + translation equivariance, RNN/SSM = sequential Markov-like compression into a fixed-size state, attention = content-addressed all-pairs lookup with an unbounded (growing) memory. Fixed-state models win on throughput/long sequences and lose on exact recall; attention wins on recall/in-context learning and loses on quadratic compute and linear KV-cache memory. Vision evidence suggests much of the "ViT beats CNN" story is a compute/data/recipe effect, not a fundamental gap.

### Cited Findings

#### MLPs (P as components; E/S as standalone backbones)
- Core idea: stacked affine maps + pointwise nonlinearity; no spatial or sequence structure built in (permutation-agnostic over input features). In modern nets MLPs survive as the per-token FFN block inside Transformers [unverified, standard knowledge].
- MLP-Mixer: all-MLP vision model alternating token-mixing MLPs (across patches) and channel-mixing MLPs; competitive but not better than ViT/CNN at scale — [MLP-Mixer 2105.01601 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01601).
- ResMLP: residual MLP for image classification with data-efficient training — [2105.03404 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03404). gMLP (spatial gating unit, "Pay Attention to MLPs") — [2105.08050 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08050).
- MetaFormer thesis: the general architecture (token mixer + channel MLP + residual/norm), not the specific attention mixer, is what matters for vision — [MetaFormer Is Actually What You Need for Vision 2111.11418 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11418).
- Spectral bias: standard ReLU MLPs learn low-frequency functions first; Fourier-feature input mappings let coordinate MLPs fit high-frequency signals — [On the Spectral Bias of Neural Networks 1806.08734 (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08734); [Fourier Features Let Networks Learn High Frequency Functions 2006.10739 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10739).
- KAN (learnable univariate functions on edges, Kolmogorov-Arnold inspiration) — [2404.19756 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19756); a controlled comparison reports KAN's advantage largely confined to symbolic-formula representation and attributable to its B-spline activations, with MLP better on other tasks (abstract-level reading; details [unverified]) — [KAN or MLP: A Fairer Comparison 2407.16674 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16674). Maturity of KAN: S/F.
- Wins: small tabular/low-dim problems, coordinate networks (with Fourier features), as FFN blocks. Loses: anything with exploitable structure at scale (images, text) when used as the only backbone.

#### CNNs (P)
- Core idea: weight-shared local filters => locality prior + translation equivariance + hierarchical receptive fields. Cost O(N * k) in input size.
- ResNet: identity skip connections made very deep nets trainable — [Deep Residual Learning 1512.03385 (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385). The residual stream idea is reused by essentially every later family.
- Dilated convolution for multi-scale context without downsampling — [Yu & Koltun 1511.07122 (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07122). Depthwise-separable convolutions (Xception, MobileNets) factor spatial and channel mixing for efficiency — [1610.02357 (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02357), [1704.04861 (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04861). EfficientNet compound scaling — [1905.11946 (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11946). RepVGG structural re-parameterization (multi-branch at train time, plain conv at inference) — [2101.03697 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03697).
- ConvNeXt: a pure ConvNet modernized with Transformer-era recipe choices (7x7 depthwise conv, LayerNorm, inverted bottleneck, GELU, AdamW etc.) competes with Swin — [A ConvNet for the 2020s 2201.03545 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03545); ConvNeXt V2 adds masked-autoencoder co-design (GRN layer) — [2301.00808 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00808).
- Scale test: NFNet-family ConvNets pretrained on JFT-4B show a log-log scaling law and match fine-tuned ViTs at comparable compute — [ConvNets Match Vision Transformers at Scale 2310.16764 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16764).
- 1-D convolutions are also the substrate of long-conv sequence models (S4/Hyena below), so "CNN" and "SSM" overlap.
- Wins: data-efficient, hardware-friendly, strong with small data and dense prediction. Loses: no native content-dependent long-range mixing; fixed receptive field unless depth/dilation grows.

#### RNNs: LSTM, GRU and the revival (E; revival F)
- Core idea: h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t); a fixed-size state compresses history. O(1) memory per step, O(N) compute, but classic nonlinear recurrence is sequential in training (cannot be parallelized over time) and suffers vanishing/exploding gradients. LSTM's cell + gates (constant error carousel) address the gradient issue; GRU is a lighter gated variant — [GRU / RNN Encoder-Decoder 1406.1078 (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.1078). Attention originated as an add-on to RNN seq2seq — [Bahdanau et al. 1409.0473 (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473).
- xLSTM: exponential gating + (i) sLSTM scalar memory with memory mixing and (ii) mLSTM with matrix memory, covariance update rule, fully parallelizable; stacked in residual blocks; claims favorable scaling versus Transformers/SSMs — [2405.04517 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04517).
- minLSTM/minGRU: drop hidden-state dependence from the gates so the recurrence becomes a linear recurrence computable by parallel scan; reported ~175x (minGRU) training speedups at length 512 vs traditional RNNs [number from abstract, not re-verified in body] — [Were RNNs All We Needed? 2410.01201 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01201).
- LRU: linear recurrences with complex diagonal parameterization, stable exponential init and normalization matched deep SSMs on Long Range Arena — [Resurrecting RNNs 1 2303.06349 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06349). Linear recurrence followed by nonlinear projection is a universal approximator in a finite-width sense — [2307.11888 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11888).
- Non-linear RNNs can be parallelized over time by Newton/fixed-point (DEER) solves — [Parallelizing non-linear sequential models 2309.12252 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12252). Recursion/"recurrent depth" revival for reasoning: [Hierarchical Reasoning Model 2506.21734 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734); [Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks 2510.04871 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871); [Universal Transformers 1807.03819 (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819); [Mixture-of-Recursions 2507.10524 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524). Maturity: F/S (reasoning claims contested [unverified]).
- Wins: constant-memory streaming inference, state tracking when nonlinear. Loses: lossy memory (cannot exactly retrieve arbitrary past tokens), historically hard to scale.

#### Transformers and attention variants (P)
- Core idea: softmax(QK^T/sqrt(d))V; every token reads from all others by content similarity. Permutation-equivariant, so position must be injected. Compute O(N^2 d), KV cache O(N d) per layer at inference. Training is fully parallel — [Attention Is All You Need 1706.03762 (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762).
- Efficient/sparse/low-rank/linear attention: Reformer (LSH, 2001.04451 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04451)); Linformer (low-rank, [2006.04768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04768)); Longformer (sliding window + global, [2004.05150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)); BigBird (window+global+random, [2007.14062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14062)); Performer (random-feature softmax approx, [2009.14794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14794)); Transformer-XL segment recurrence ([1901.02860](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02860)); Block-Recurrent Transformers ([2203.07852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07852)); MEGA moving-average gated attention ([2209.10655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10655)). Benchmark: [Long Range Arena 2011.04006 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04006). Observation [unverified, general knowledge]: most approximate-attention variants lost out in practice to exact attention made fast by hardware-aware kernels.
- FlashAttention: exact attention, IO-aware tiling to avoid materializing the N x N matrix in HBM (memory linear in N, compute still quadratic) — [2205.14135 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135); better work partitioning — [FlashAttention-2 2307.08691 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08691); Hopper asynchrony/low-precision — [FlashAttention-3 2407.08608 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08608). Ring Attention shards sequence across devices — [2310.01889 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01889).
- KV-cache reduction: Multi-Query Attention [1911.02150 (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02150), Grouped-Query Attention [2305.13245 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13245), Multi-head Latent Attention (introduced in DeepSeek-V2) [2405.04434 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434). Attention sinks and streaming with a sink + window cache — [StreamingLLM 2309.17453 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453). Trained sparse attention in production-scale models: [Native Sparse Attention 2502.11089 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11089); DeepSeek Sparse Attention (lightning indexer + fine-grained token selection on top of MLA) in [DeepSeek-V3.2 2512.02556 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556).
- Modifications of the softmax itself: Differential Transformer (difference of two softmax maps to cancel noise) [2410.05258 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05258); gated attention output (sigmoid gate after SDPA) reported to add non-linearity, sparsity and remove attention sinks [Gated Attention for LLMs 2505.06708 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06708). Attention as dense associative memory (modern Hopfield) [2008.02217 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217).
- Positional schemes: RoPE rotates q/k by position-dependent angle so q·k depends on relative offset — [RoFormer 2104.09864 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864); ALiBi linear distance bias, extrapolates to longer inputs — [2108.12409 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409); Position Interpolation to extend RoPE context — [2306.15595 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595); YaRN — [2309.00071 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071); finding that no explicit position encoding (NoPE) can generalize to longer lengths better than several explicit schemes in decoder-only models — [2305.19466 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466). Context use is imperfect: models use the start/end of the context far better than the middle — [Lost in the Middle 2307.03172 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172).
- Vision Transformers: patchify image into tokens, plain Transformer; needs large data (weaker image priors than CNN) — [ViT 2010.11929 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929); data-efficient training/distillation [DeiT 2012.12877 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12877); hierarchical shifted windows [Swin 2103.14030 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030); scaling behavior [Scaling Vision Transformers 2106.04560 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04560).
- Wins: in-context learning, exact copy/retrieval, massively parallel training, scales smoothly with data/compute. Loses: quadratic compute and linear-memory KV cache for long context; no recurrence so some serial computations (state tracking) need depth/CoT.

#### State-space and recurrent-scan models (E -> production in hybrids)
- Core idea: a linear time-invariant system x'(t)=Ax+Bu, y=Cx+Du discretized to a linear recurrence; can be run as a recurrence (O(1)/step), a long convolution (training), or via parallel scan. HiPPO supplies the A matrix that provably compresses history onto orthogonal polynomials — [HiPPO 2008.07669 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07669); LSSL unified RNN/CNN/continuous-time views — [2110.13985 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13985).
- S4: structured (diagonal-plus-low-rank) A for stable, efficient training on length 10k+ — [2111.00396 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396); simplification to diagonal: [DSS 2203.14343 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14343), [S4D 2206.11893 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11893); multi-input multi-output with parallel scan: [S5 2208.04933 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04933). Caveat: LRA gaps between SSMs and Transformers shrink or vanish when Transformers are pretrained on the task data — [Never Train from Scratch 2310.02980 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02980).
- LTI models cannot do content-based selection; H3 added shift/diag SSM with multiplicative interactions to emulate recall/compare [Hungry Hungry Hippos 2212.14052 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14052); Hyena replaces attention with implicitly-parametrized long convolutions + data-controlled gating, subquadratic — [2302.10866 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10866).
- Mamba: makes B, C and the step size Delta input-dependent ("selective"), giving content-based gating/forgetting; loses convolutional form so uses a hardware-aware scan; linear time — [2312.00752 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752). Mamba-2 simplifies A to scalar-times-identity per head enabling matmul-based chunked algorithms, 2-8x faster, via SSD duality — [2405.21060 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060). Mamba-3 (ICLR 2026): exponential-trapezoidal discretization, complex-valued state transitions (solves state-tracking tasks Mamba-2 cannot), MIMO formulation for hardware efficiency; comparable perplexity to Mamba-2 at half the state size; at 1.5B it reports +0.6 points average downstream accuracy over Gated DeltaNet and +1.8 with MIMO — [2603.15569 (Mar 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15569) (figures from search summary of abstract; also [ICLR 2026 page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010352)).
- RWKV: attention-free "RNN at inference, Transformer-like at train" — [RWKV 2305.13048 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13048); matrix-valued states/dynamic recurrence — [Eagle and Finch 2404.05892 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05892); RWKV-7 "Goose": generalized delta rule with vector-valued gating and in-context learning rates; shown to do state tracking and recognize all regular languages with constant memory and per-token time — [2503.14456 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14456).
- RetNet: retention mechanism with parallel, recurrent and chunkwise-recurrent forms (O(1) inference) — [2307.08621 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08621).
- Gated linear attention and delta-rule family (the linear-attention branch of the same space): GLA — [2312.06635 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06635); DeltaNet parallelized over sequence length — [2406.06484 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484); Gated DeltaNet (gating for fast erasure + delta rule for targeted updates; beats Mamba2/DeltaNet on LM, retrieval, long context) — [2412.06464 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464); Based (linear attention + sliding window, recall-throughput tradeoff) — [2402.18668 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18668); log-linear attention (state grows logarithmically) — [2506.04761 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04761); Longhorn (SSM as online learner) — [2407.14207 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14207).
- Memory-as-test-time-learning branch: TTT layers (hidden state is a model updated by self-supervised gradient steps) — [2407.04620 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620); Titans (neural long-term memory + attention as short-term memory) — [2501.00663 (2024-12)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663); ATLAS — [2505.23735 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23735). Maturity: F/S.
- Vision SSMs: [Vision Mamba 2401.09417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09417), [VMamba 2401.10166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10166) (both 2024); maturity E, not clearly dominant over ConvNeXt/ViT/Swin [unverified].
- Wins: long sequences, constant-memory decoding, high throughput; strong on signal-like/continuous data (LRA). Loses: exact recall/copy and retrieval-heavy in-context tasks unless hybridized (see Q2).

### Inferences
- Inductive-bias ordering by "memory growth": CNN (fixed local window) < RNN/SSM (fixed-size state) < log-linear/TTT-style (state growing slowly or learned) < attention (memory grows with N). Almost every 2024-2026 innovation is a way to interpolate on this axis.
- The vision data shows that bias matters most when data/compute are small and washes out with scale; language shows an analogous pattern in which weaker-bias attention wins on recall.

### Gaps
- No independent verification of exact speedup figures in abstracts beyond what the API text shows (e.g., minGRU 175x, Mamba-2 2-8x quoted from abstract text only partially).
- ViT-vs-ConvNet head-to-head after 2023 (e.g., against modern recipe ViTs at JFT-scale) not surveyed.
- Not covered: MoE FFNs, normalization/optimizer interactions, Perceiver/Set Transformer, graph networks (out of assigned scope).

## Q2. Current (2025-2026) state of the Transformer vs SSM/recurrent debate; hybrids; known failure modes

### Takeaway
Pure attention remains the default for frontier labs, pure SSMs lose on recall, and the production consensus has drifted toward hybrids with a small fraction of (often gated) full-attention layers plus linear-RNN layers (Gated DeltaNet/Mamba-2/KDA), but a prominent counter-datapoint (MiniMax-M2 reverting to full attention) shows the evidence at scale is not settled; sparse attention (DSA, NSA) is a competing efficiency path that keeps softmax attention.

### Cited Findings
- Recall failure mode, theory: fixed-size state models cannot copy long strings; a 2-layer Transformer can copy exponentially long strings, and pretrained Transformers beat pretrained SSMs on copy/retrieval — [Repeat After Me 2402.01032 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032).
- Recall failure mode, empirics: across 17 models, gated-convolution/attention-free models trail attention by up to 2.1 perplexity points on the Pile, 82% of the gap explained by associative recall ("Hakuna Matata" in-context lookup) — [Zoology 2312.04927 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927). Based shows an explicit recall-throughput tradeoff in state size — [2402.18668 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18668).
- At 8B scale in a controlled setting, Mamba/Mamba-2 pure models were compared with Transformers and hybrids by NVIDIA (conclusions: hybrid helps; abstract partially read; details [unverified]) — [An Empirical Study of Mamba-based Language Models 2406.07887 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07887). Mamba on in-context-learning tasks: competitive on many but weaker on some (details [unverified]) — [Can Mamba Learn How to Learn? 2402.04248 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04248).
- State tracking: SSMs (S4/Mamba) are in TC0, like Transformers, so they cannot do permutation composition in one pass, contradicting the intuition that recurrence is automatically stronger — [Illusion of State 2404.08819 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819); log-precision Transformers are limited by parallelism [Parallelism Tradeoff 2207.00729 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00729); chain of thought raises Transformer expressivity — [2310.07923 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07923). Fix: allow negative eigenvalues (range [-1,1]) in the diagonal/DeltaNet transition so linear RNNs can solve parity and more — [2411.12537 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537); Mamba-3 uses complex states for the same end — [2603.15569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15569); RWKV-7 generalized delta rule recognizes all regular languages — [2503.14456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14456).
- Hybrids, production/near-production lineage: Griffin/Hawk (gated linear recurrence + local attention; Griffin matches Llama-2 with >6x fewer tokens) [2402.19427 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19427), RecurrentGemma [2404.07839 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07839); Jamba (Transformer-Mamba-MoE interleave, 80GB single-GPU fit) [2403.19887 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19887) and Jamba-1.5 [2408.12570 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12570); Samba (Mamba + sliding-window attention) [2406.07522 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07522); Zamba [2405.16712 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16712); Hymba (parallel attention+SSM heads) [2411.13676 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13676); Nemotron-H (majority of attention layers replaced by Mamba layers) [2504.03624 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03624); Falcon-H1 (hybrid-head) [2507.22448 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22448); MiniMax-01 (Lightning Attention hybrid) [2501.08313 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08313).
- Kimi Linear: layerwise hybrid of Kimi Delta Attention (finer-grained gating extending Gated DeltaNet, specialized DPLR chunkwise kernel) and MLA; claims to be the first hybrid linear architecture outperforming full attention under fair comparisons in short-context, long-context and RL-scaling regimes (3B active/48B total) — [2510.26692 (Oct 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692).
- Qwen3-Next and Qwen3.5: 3 Gated DeltaNet layers per 1 gated full-attention layer (Qwen3-Next 80B-A3B: 48 layers = 12 repeats; 36 linear, 12 softmax); Alibaba reports ~7x throughput at 4K context and >10x beyond 32K vs Qwen3-32B — [Raschka architecture gallery (secondary)](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/hybrid-attention/); same 3:1 pattern carried into Qwen3.5 (flagship 397B-A17B) per [Labonne (secondary)](https://huggingface.co/blog/mlabonne/qwen35). Official Qwen model cards not fetched.
- Systematic ablations: 72 models (340M/20B tokens and 1.3B/100B tokens), six linear-attention variants x five hybrid ratios: language-modeling quality roughly flat across ratios but recall depends strongly on the ratio, with gains below roughly 3:1 linear:full; recommends gated-delta-type or HGRN-2 at 3:1 to 6:1 — [A Systematic Analysis of Hybrid Linear Attention 2507.06457 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06457). Meta FAIR's inter-layer vs intra-layer (parallel) hybrid study: hybrids beat homogeneous on long-context retrieval/downstream accuracy — [Hybrid Architectures for Language Models 2510.04800 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04800) (abstract-level; search summary).
- Counter-datapoint: MiniMax, after M1/01 Lightning-Attention hybrids, shipped M2 as full attention; team says small-scale hybrid tests looked equal but linear/sliding-window variants showed reasoning deficits at larger scale, infrastructure (prefix caching, speculative decoding, numerical precision) is less mature, and frames it as pragmatic, not a rejection; critics note Kimi Linear's counter-evidence (with possibly weak baselines) and that M2's constraints are implementation not fundamental — [MiniMax post](https://www.minimax.io/news/why-did-m2-end-up-as-a-full-attention-model), [HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/blog/MiniMax-AI/why-did-m2-end-up-as-a-full-attention-model), critique [Kaitchup](https://kaitchup.substack.com/p/minimax-m2-and-kimi-linear-why-full). The "severe reasoning deficits" detail came via VentureBeat's summary of the technical report; I did not fetch the report [unverified]. A MiniMax M3 with its own sparse attention is reported by secondary coverage only [unverified].
- Sparse-attention path: NSA [2502.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11089), DSA in DeepSeek-V3.2 [2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556); the DSA is the only architectural change from V3.1-Terminus, added through continued training (per paper/search summary).
- Quality-of-attention fixes that matter for hybrids: attention sinks / massive activations; gated attention output removes them [2505.06708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06708).

### Inferences
- Rough consensus design rule: 3:1 to ~7:1 linear-to-softmax layer ratio with a delta-rule/gated linear layer. Evidence is mostly from labs reporting their own comparisons; independent replications are scarce (2507.06457 and 2510.04800 are the independent-ish ones).
- The honest summary of the debate: the question has moved from "which wins" to "how few softmax layers can you keep, and is the serving stack (prefix cache, speculative decoding, quantization) ready". MiniMax's retreat shows infra maturity may dominate architecture merit for production decisions.
- Retrieval/copy limits are a fixed-state capacity law, so any pure fixed-state model will lose there; hybrids avoid it by keeping some unbounded-memory layers, and TTT/Titans/log-linear try to grow state.

### Gaps
- No fetched primary source for Qwen3-Next/Qwen3.5 layer ratios (used secondary); no fetched MiniMax M2 technical report.
- Few fair, large-scale (>10B, >1T tokens), independent comparisons of Transformer vs hybrid vs sparse-attention at equal compute; Kimi Linear's "outperforms full attention" is self-reported.
- Not checked: Mamba-3 downstream real-world adoption, Granite 4, Hope/Nested Learning, any 2026 hybrid scaling-law papers beyond search hits.

## Q3. Unifying views

### Takeaway
Linear attention, SSMs, RNNs, and fast-weight programmers are the same object (a recurrent state updated by outer-product-like rules and read by a query), differing in the update rule (decay, gating, delta rule, gradient step) and the parameterization of the transition. Softmax attention is the infinite-state kernel/associative-memory limit.

### Cited Findings
- Linear attention as RNN: replacing softmax by a kernel feature map lets causal attention be computed as an RNN with O(1) per step state S_t = S_{t-1} + phi(k_t) v_t^T — [Transformers are RNNs 2006.16236 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16236).
- Linear attention = fast weight programmers (1991 Schmidhuber lineage): the state is a fast weight matrix written with an outer-product (Hebbian) rule; the delta rule and other fixes follow — [Linear Transformers Are Secretly Fast Weight Programmers 2102.11174 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174).
- State space duality: SSMs and attention variants are connected through structured semiseparable matrices; Mamba-2's layer is the scalar-A case, giving matmul-friendly algorithms — [Transformers are SSMs 2405.21060 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060). Same decomposition gives chunkwise (blocked) algorithms used by GLA/DeltaNet/GDN — [2312.06635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06635), [2406.06484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484).
- Test-time regression: sequence layers viewed as associative memory solving an online regression problem, unifying linear attention, SSMs, DeltaNet, softmax attention (as a nonparametric regressor) — [2501.12352 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352). Online-learning views: Longhorn [2407.14207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14207); TTT [2407.04620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620) (state = model, update = gradient step); Titans [2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663).
- Attention as dense associative memory (modern Hopfield) — [2008.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217). Attention as an RNN with many-to-one / parallel prefix scan — [Attention as an RNN 2405.13956 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13956). Decoder-only Transformers as "multi-state RNNs" with unbounded state, compressible by KV eviction — [2401.06104 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06104).
- RetNet: explicit parallel/recurrent/chunkwise triple form — [2307.08621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08621). Parallel scan makes non-diagonal/gated linear recurrences train-parallel (S5 [2208.04933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04933), minGRU [2410.01201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01201)). Long-conv view: S4/Hyena [2111.00396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396), [2302.10866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10866).
- Architecture-level unification: MetaFormer (token mixer + channel mixer is the real design unit) — [2111.11418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11418); Transformer FFN, SSM, conv, linear attention are all choices of token mixer.

### Inferences
- A useful axis grid for novelty search: (state size growth: fixed / log / linear) x (state update: additive, decayed, delta-rule, gradient-step, nonlinear) x (transition structure: scalar, diagonal, DPLR, dense/orthogonal, negative/complex eigenvalues) x (readout: linear, softmax, MLP) x (hybridization: interleave, parallel heads, local window). Most published models occupy a few cells; e.g., second-order/nonlinear updates with hardware-efficient scans, mixing log-linear state growth with delta-rule, and recurrence-depth for reasoning are comparatively less explored [unverified against exhaustive search].
- Duality results are about algebra of the sequence mixer, not about trained-model equivalence: equal expressiveness classes (TC0) do not mean equal learnability or recall.

### Gaps
- No search of unifying frameworks beyond those above (e.g., Hopfield/energy-based, kernel-machine views of deep SSMs) was exhaustive.

## Q4. Open problems and recent surprising results

### Takeaway
The open problems are: exact recall vs constant memory, state tracking in parallel-trainable recurrences, fair evaluation (benchmarks that over- or underestimate architecture gaps), serving-infrastructure maturity, and whether linear/hybrid models retain reasoning quality under long RL-trained chain-of-thought.

### Cited Findings
- Surprise 1: SSMs' expressive state-tracking advantage is an illusion for diagonal-nonnegative designs — [2404.08819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819); partially repaired by negative eigenvalues — [2411.12537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537).
- Surprise 2: Pretraining on task data closes much of the LRA gap between SSMs and Transformers, so benchmark gains were partly an initialization effect — [2310.02980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02980).
- Surprise 3: Vision: ConvNets match ViTs at scale given equal compute — [2310.16764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16764); MetaFormer — [2111.11418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11418).
- Surprise 4: Language modeling perplexity can be insensitive to hybrid ratio while recall varies strongly — [2507.06457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06457) (so perplexity is a poor selection signal for efficient architectures; also Zoology [2312.04927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927)).
- Surprise 5: Small, recursive networks reported to beat large LLMs on hard puzzle benchmarks (ARC-AGI, Sudoku, Maze) — [Tiny Recursive Models 2510.04871 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871), [HRM 2506.21734 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734); scope narrow and generalization claims contested [unverified].
- Surprise 6: positional information can be learned without explicit encoding in causal decoders (NoPE) — [2305.19466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466); attention-sink phenomenon is architectural (softmax normalization) and fixable with gating — [2309.17453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453), [2505.06708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06708).
- Open: long-context use is poor even for full attention (middle of context ignored) — [Lost in the Middle 2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172).
- Open: hybrid and linear models hit infrastructure limits (prefix caching, speculative decoding, low-precision sensitivity) — [MiniMax](https://www.minimax.io/news/why-did-m2-end-up-as-a-full-attention-model).
- Open (kernels): efficient hardware kernels are the gating factor for every new recurrence (Mamba hardware-aware scan [2312.00752], Mamba-2 SSD [2405.21060], KDA's DPLR variant [2510.26692], Mamba-3 MIMO [2603.15569]).
- Open: KAN-style learnable activations remain unproven beyond symbolic regression — [2407.16674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16674).

### Inferences
- Promising combination directions suggested by the evidence (all hypotheses, not findings): (a) delta-rule layers with negative/complex eigenvalues plus few gated softmax layers (already near what Qwen3-Next/Mamba-3 point to); (b) sparse/selective attention layers in place of softmax layers inside hybrids (DSA/NSA x GDN); (c) state that grows sublinearly or is adaptively compressed (log-linear, Titans/ATLAS) to move off the recall-memory frontier; (d) recursive depth for reasoning tasks on top of linear recurrent mixers; (e) using vision-style evidence (recipe > family) to insist on fair-recipe baselines in any new-architecture claim.
- Evaluation advice: require recall/retrieval probes (Zoology/associative recall, copy) and state-tracking probes (parity, permutation composition) alongside perplexity; compare against well-tuned, equally-compute baselines; test long RL/CoT regimes.

### Gaps
- Could not verify any 2026 results beyond Mamba-3; searches surfaced arXiv ids with 2607/2608 prefixes (e.g., "Qwen3.8-Next" 2608.30320, massive activations in hybrids 2608.12149) whose titles looked unreliable or I did not fetch — not used.
- No quantitative reconciliation between Kimi Linear's "beats full attention" and MiniMax's "full attention needed" claims.
- Did not cover sparse-MoE, diffusion LMs, or multimodal fusion (out of assigned scope).
