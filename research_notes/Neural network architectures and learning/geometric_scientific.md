## Q1. How is symmetry/structure built in (equivariance, invariance, conservation laws, PDE residuals), and what is gained or lost?

### Takeaway
Structure enters in four ways: (a) by architecture (weight sharing over a group: CNN, G-CNN, steerable, gauge, E(3)-equivariant message passing); (b) by the form of the output (energy-then-gradient for conservative forces, Hamiltonian-then-symplectic-gradient for dynamics); (c) by the loss (PDE residuals in PINNs); (d) by data augmentation (soft symmetry). You gain data efficiency and guaranteed consistency; you lose flexibility, speed/hardware efficiency, and sometimes accuracy at scale, and 2024-2026 evidence says hard equivariance is no longer automatically the winning bet when data and compute are abundant.

### Cited Findings

**Unifying framework**
- Geometric Deep Learning "proto-book" (Bronstein, Bruna, Cohen, Veličković): organises grids, groups, graphs, geodesics, gauges under symmetry + scale separation blueprint. Verified [2104.13478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13478) (2021). Maturity: canonical reference, not a method.

**Graph neural networks (GNNs)**
- *Plain idea:* each node repeatedly replaces its feature vector with a learned function of its own feature and a permutation-invariant aggregate (sum/mean/max/attention) of neighbours' features. Permutation equivariance on nodes is built in. *Precise:* message passing h_v' = U(h_v, AGG_{u in N(v)} M(h_v, h_u, e_uv)); the MPNN formulation unifies earlier variants. [from background knowledge, not re-verified]
- GCN (Kipf & Welling): spectral-motivated first-order propagation with a normalised adjacency. Verified [1609.02907](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907) (2016).
- GAT (Veličković et al.): learned attention coefficients over neighbours. Verified [1710.10903](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10903) (2017).
- MPNN (Gilmer et al.): "Neural Message Passing for Quantum Chemistry", the general framework, evaluated on QM9. Verified [1704.01212](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.01212) (2017).
- Expressivity ceiling: GIN (Xu et al.) "How Powerful are GNNs?" verified [1810.00826](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00826) (2018); Morris et al. "Weisfeiler and Leman Go Neural" verified [1810.02244](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02244) (2018). Standard message passing is at most as discriminative as the 1-WL colour-refinement test [from background knowledge, not re-verified].
- Graph transformers: Dwivedi & Bresson [2012.09699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09699) (2020); Graphormer (Ying et al.) [2106.05234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05234) (2021); GPS "Recipe for a General, Powerful, Scalable Graph Transformer" (Rampášek et al.) [2205.12454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12454) (2022). Idea: global attention with structural/positional encodings so that distant nodes interact in one layer, at O(N^2) cost unless sparsified. All verified.
- Oversmoothing: Li, Han, Wu "Deeper Insights into GCNs" [1801.07606](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07606) (2018) (Laplacian smoothing view); Oono & Suzuki "GNNs Exponentially Lose Expressive Power for Node Classification" [1905.10947](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10947) (2019). Verified. Intuition: repeated averaging drives node features to a graph-determined fixed point, so depth hurts.
- Oversquashing: Alon & Yahav [2006.05205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05205) (2020) (exponentially many neighbours squeezed into a fixed-size vector); Topping et al. curvature view [2111.14522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14522) (2021); Di Giovanni et al. "How does over-squashing affect the power of GNNs?" [2306.03589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03589) (2023). All verified.
- Surveys: over-squashing survey 2308.15568 and rewiring survey 2411.17429 **[id via search snippet]**; the rewiring survey states that preprocessing rewiring can scale badly and may amplify oversmoothing — [Attali et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17429).
- **Critique of the graph-transformer advantage:** Tönshoff et al. "Where Did the Gap Go? Reassessing the Long-Range Graph Benchmark" reports that the reported transformer-over-GNN gap largely disappears after basic hyperparameter tuning — [2309.00367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00367) **[id via search snippet]**. A NeurIPS 2024 follow-up (Luo, Shi, Wu, "Classic GNNs are strong baselines") reports tuned GCN/GAT/GraphSAGE match or beat graph transformers on 17 of 18 node-classification datasets; extension to graph-level tasks is arXiv 2502.09263 — all via [search results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09263) **[ids via search snippet; Luo et al. node-classification arXiv id not retrieved]**.
- Maturity: GNN message passing = production (recommenders, molecules, weather); graph transformers = established research; rewiring = frontier/contested.
- Science wins: GraphCast, a GNN weather emulator on an icosahedral multimesh. Verified [2212.12794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12794) (2022). Medium-range skill vs ECMWF HRES is the paper's claim [from background knowledge, not re-verified]. Also MACE-MP-0 below.

**Equivariant networks: group CNNs to E(3) message passing**
- Group CNN (Cohen & Welling): convolution over a discrete group (rotations/flips) via filter-transformation weight sharing. Verified [1602.07576](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07576) (2016). Steerable CNNs [1612.08498](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08498) (2016): features are fields transforming under group representations; filters are constrained to be equivariant (steerability). Gauge equivariant CNNs / icosahedral CNN [1902.04615](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04615) (2019): convolution on manifolds where the kernel must be equivariant to local frame (gauge) choice. All verified.
- Tensor field networks (Thomas et al.) [1802.08219](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08219) (2018): filters = radial function x spherical harmonics, features = irreducible representations of SO(3), combined via Clebsch-Gordan tensor products. SE(3)-Transformers [2006.10503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10503) (2020). e3nn library [2207.09453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09453) (2022). All verified.
- Invariant (not equivariant) predecessors: SchNet [1706.08566](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08566) (2017, distances only); DimeNet [2003.03123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03123) (2020, adds angles). Verified.
- EGNN (Satorras et al.) [2102.09844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09844) (2021): E(n)-equivariant updates using only invariant scalars (squared distances) and vector updates along relative position vectors — cheap, no spherical harmonics. Verified.
- NequIP [2101.03164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03164) (2021): E(3)-equivariant interatomic potential; claims data efficiency (accurate with orders of magnitude fewer training structures) [from background knowledge, not re-verified]. Allegro [2204.05249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05249) (2022): strictly local equivariant model for large-scale MD. MACE [2206.07697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07697) (2022): higher-body-order equivariant messages (ACE-style) in few layers. MACE-MP-0 "A foundation model for atomistic materials chemistry" [2401.00096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00096) (2023): a pretrained universal potential across the periodic table. All verified.
- Maturity: equivariant MLIPs = production-grade in computational chemistry/materials (foundation/universal potentials now routine); SE(3)-transformers/gauge nets = established-to-niche; EGNN-style = widely used generative/molecule baseline.
- **What is lost / does equivariance still matter at scale?** Brehmer et al. "Does equivariance matter at scale?" [2410.23179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23179) (2024, verified). I did not read the body this session; the title frames the question and the paper's reported answer (equivariant models are more compute-efficient and data-efficient than non-equivariant ones with augmentation, but a non-equivariant transformer scaled up can partially close the gap) **[unverified — read before citing the conclusion]**. Adjacent 2026 results found via arXiv search (title/author verified only): "Turbulence teaches equivariance to neural networks" [2602.04695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04695) (McConkey, 2026) — suggests equivariance can be *learned from data* rather than imposed **[claim from title; unverified]**.
- **Conservative vs direct force prediction (a concrete structure trade-off):** Bigi, Langer, Ceriotti "The dark side of the forces: assessing non-conservative force models for atomistic machine learning" (ICML 2025) — direct force heads are faster but break energy conservation, causing ill-defined geometry-optimisation convergence and MD instabilities; recommended recipe is to pretrain on direct forces and fine-tune with backprop forces — [2412.11569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11569) **[id via search snippet]**. Also eSEN, "Learning Smooth and Expressive Interatomic Potentials" [2502.12147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12147) (Fu et al., 2025, verified title) — claims smoothness/conservativeness matter for downstream property prediction **[claim from title; unverified]**.

**Hamiltonian / Lagrangian networks (structure via the form of the dynamics)**
- HNN (Greydanus et al.) [1906.01563](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01563) (2019): learn scalar H(q,p) with a network and obtain time derivatives from Hamilton's equations by autodiff, so energy is conserved by construction (to the extent the learned H is the true one) [from background knowledge, not re-verified]. LNN (Cranmer et al.) [2003.04630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04630) (2020): learn the Lagrangian, derive accelerations through the Euler-Lagrange equations, needing no canonical momenta. Deep Lagrangian Networks (Lutter et al.) [1907.04490](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) (2019). Symplectic ODE-Net [1909.12077](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12077) (2019), SympNets [2001.03750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03750) (2020, intrinsically symplectic layers). Neural ODE [1806.07366](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07366) (2018) as the underlying continuous-depth tool. Finzi et al. "Simplifying Hamiltonian and Lagrangian NNs via Explicit Constraints" [2010.13581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13581) (2020, Cartesian coordinates + constraints). All verified.
- Benchmark/critique: Zhong, Dey, Chakraborty "Benchmarking Energy-Conserving Neural Networks for Learning Dynamics from Data" [2012.02334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02334) (2020, verified id/title). Its conclusions (I did not read them) **[unverified]**.
- Maturity: established research on low-dimensional, clean, noise-free systems (pendulums, N-body toys); not production. Known weakness class (from background knowledge, **[unverified in this session]**): need for derivatives or accurate integration at training, sensitivity to noise and to coordinate choice, dissipation/friction not captured by pure H, no guarantee the learned H is physically unique (gauge freedom of H by constants/Casimirs).

### Inferences
- Equivariance buys correctness-by-construction exactly where the symmetry is exact and the data are scarce (molecular energies, point clouds). Where data are abundant, symmetry can partly be learned, and hardware-friendly dense architectures can win; the practical decision is a compute-vs-data trade-off, not a dogma.
- "Conservation by output form" (energy->force; H->flow) provides a hard guarantee on a *derived* quantity and moves the modelling burden to a smooth scalar, which is why conservative MLIPs behave better in long MD than direct-force heads.
- The graph-transformer vs GNN story is a cautionary tale about baseline tuning, which recurs for neural operators and PINNs (Q2).

- Did not read the body of 2410.23179 or 2412.11569/2602.04695, so exact quantitative scaling conclusions are not stated here.

- AlphaFold 2/3 use equivariance-flavoured modules (IPA, then a non-equivariant diffusion module in AF3) — a Nature DOI check was not done, so I make no claim.

## Q2. Where do PINNs and KANs genuinely help, and where do independent benchmarks show them underperforming? (plus neural operators)

### Takeaway
Neural operators (FNO/DeepONet/transformer operators) are an established way to amortise solves over parameter or initial-condition families and have real wins in speed once trained, but a systematic review finds weak baselines and reporting bias in the fluid-PDE ML literature. PINNs are useful mainly for inverse/data-assimilation and irregular-geometry problems; for forward solves they are generally beaten by classical solvers and fail on stiff/multi-scale/chaotic problems unless heavily engineered. KANs show an advantage mainly in symbolic-formula fitting and small interpretable science problems; parameter/FLOP-matched comparisons mostly erase their gains elsewhere.

### Cited Findings

**Neural operators**
- Idea: learn a map between function spaces (e.g., coefficient field -> solution field) so one trained network is a fast surrogate across a family of PDE instances. Formal framework: Kovachki et al. "Neural Operator: Learning Maps Between Function Spaces" verified [2108.08481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08481) (2021).
- FNO (Li et al.): kernel integral operator parameterised in Fourier space — FFT, multiply a truncated set of low modes by learned complex weights, inverse FFT, plus a pointwise linear skip. Verified [2010.08895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895) (2020). Resolution-invariant in principle; naturally suited to regular grids/periodic domains [from background knowledge, not re-verified].
- DeepONet (Lu et al.): branch net encodes the input function sampled at sensor points, trunk net encodes the query coordinate, output = their inner product; motivated by the universal approximation theorem for operators. Verified [1910.03193](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03193) (2019).
- Transformers/general geometry: Galerkin Transformer (Cao) [2105.14995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14995) (2021); GNOT [2302.14376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14376) (2023); Transolver (physics-aware tokens by soft slice assignment, linear attention) [2402.02366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02366) (2024); UPT [2402.12365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12365) (2024); CNO (Convolutional Neural Operator, addresses aliasing / continuous-discrete equivalence) [2302.01178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01178) (2023). All verified.
- Foundation-model direction: Poseidon [2405.19101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19101) (2024); DPOT [2403.03542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03542) (2024). Verified. Maturity: frontier.
- Benchmarks: PDEBench [2210.07182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07182) (2022); PDE-Arena "Towards Multi-spatiotemporal-scale Generalized PDE Modeling" [2209.15616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15616) (2022); The Well [2412.00568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00568) (2024). Verified.
- **Independent critique:** McGreivy & Hakim, "Weak baselines and reporting biases lead to overoptimism in machine learning for fluid-related PDEs" verified [2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07218) (2024); published Nature Machine Intelligence 6(10), DOI 10.1038/s42256-024-00897-5 — [Princeton/OSTI record via search](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/2475825-weak-baselines-reporting-biases-lead-overoptimism-machine-learning-fluid-related-partial-differential-equations). Systematic review concluding that comparisons are often biased in favour of ML. The specific numeric findings (share of papers with weak baselines etc.) were not extracted — **[unverified]**.
- Maturity: FNO/DeepONet = established research with production use in weather/CFD surrogates; operator foundation models = frontier. Typical failure modes [from background knowledge, not re-verified]: poor generalisation outside the training distribution of coefficients/geometry, spectral bias and truncation of high modes (shocks, turbulence), error accumulation in autoregressive rollout, aliasing on non-uniform meshes.

**PINNs (loss = data misfit + PDE residual at collocation points via autodiff)**
- Origin: Raissi, Perdikaris, Karniadakis "Physics Informed Deep Learning" Part I [1711.10561](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10561) and Part II [1711.10566](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10566) (2017, verified); consolidated in J. Comput. Phys. 2019 (DOI not checked). Review: Karniadakis et al., Nature Reviews Physics 2021 — URL pattern nature.com/articles/s42254-021-00314-5 found in search, DOI **[not confirmed, verify before citing]**.
- Failure-mode literature (all verified ids): Wang, Teng, Perdikaris, gradient pathologies (loss-term gradient imbalance) [2001.04536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04536) (2020); Wang, Yu, Perdikaris, NTK analysis "When and why PINNs fail to train" [2007.14527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14527) (2020); Wang et al. Fourier-feature networks / spectral bias for multi-scale PDEs [2012.10047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10047) (2020); Krishnapriyan et al. "Characterizing possible failure modes" — the loss landscape itself is hard even for simple convection/reaction problems [2109.01050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01050) (2021); Wang et al. causal training for time-dependent problems [2203.07404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07404) (2022); Rathore et al. loss-landscape perspective, ill-conditioning of the residual loss, role of second-order optimisers [2402.01868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01868) (2024).
- Fixes: Wang et al. "An Expert's Guide to Training PINNs" [2308.08468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08468) (2023); PirateNets [2402.00326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00326) (2024); Müller & Zeinhofer et al., function-space geometry / natural-gradient optimisation position paper [2402.07318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07318) (2024); Jnini et al. Gauss-Newton natural gradient for PINN CFD [2402.10680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10680) (2024).
- Benchmarks: PINNacle [2306.08827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08827) (2023, verified id/title) — ~20 PDE benchmark of PINN variants; its specific findings not read **[unverified]**. Grossmann et al., "Can PINNs beat the Finite Element Method?" [2302.04107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04107) (2023, verified id/title); the paper's answer is, in my recollection, "generally not for forward solves in the cases tested, except possibly in high dimension/special cases" **[from background knowledge, not re-verified]**.
- Maturity: established research and widely used for inverse problems, data assimilation, and as a regulariser; not competitive as a general forward PDE solver. Where genuinely helpful: sparse/noisy data plus known PDE (inverse coefficient identification), no mesh generation, high-dimensional or parametric problems; Raissi et al. emphasised data-driven discovery (Part II).

**KANs**
- Idea: put learnable univariate functions (B-splines) on edges and sum at nodes — inspired by the Kolmogorov–Arnold representation theorem — instead of fixed activations on nodes with linear weights. Original: Liu et al. "KAN: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks" verified [2404.19756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19756) (2024). Follow-up "KAN 2.0: KAN Meet Science" [2408.10205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10205) (2024, verified): tooling for feature identification, modular structure, symbolic formula discovery.
- Independent assessments:
  - Yu et al. "KAN or MLP: A Fairer Comparison" verified [2407.16674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16674) (2024): at matched parameters/FLOPs, KAN wins only on symbolic-formula representation; MLP stays ahead on ML, vision, NLP, audio — [abstract as quoted in search result](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16674v2).
  - Hou, Ji, Zhang, Stefanidis, "KAN: A Critical Assessment of Claims, Performance, and Practical Viability" — arXiv 2407.11075 (v8 Sep 2025) **[id and authors via search snippet only]**; argues the theorem-to-architecture link is weak.
  - Shukla et al. "comprehensive and FAIR comparison between MLP and KAN for differential equations and operator networks" [2406.02917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02917) (2024, id/title verified; findings **[unverified]**).
  - Li, "KANs are Radial Basis Function Networks" [2405.06721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06721) (2024, verified title): FastKAN with Gaussian RBF replaces B-splines — i.e., a re-parameterisation to known RBF networks, speeds up training (the speed claim is from background knowledge, **[unverified]**).
  - Tabular benchmark (Poeta & Giobergia, 2406.14529): KAN competitive but costlier — **[id via search snippet]**; small-LM replacement study (Alves & Vicente, July 2026, 2607.15525): no consistent advantage over parameter-matched MLPs — **[id via search snippet; authors/date from search summary only]**.
- Scientific variants: Kolmogorov-Arnold informed NN (KINN) [2406.11045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11045) (2024, verified title); Kolmogorov-Arnold Transformer [2409.10594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10594) (2024, verified).
- Maturity: frontier/speculative as general architecture; small-data symbolic science niche is the defensible use.

### Inferences
- A recurring pattern across GNN transformers, neural operators, PINNs, and KANs: headline wins shrink under matched tuning/compute and strong classical baselines; treat any single-paper SOTA claim with suspicion until a baseline-matched benchmark exists.
- PINN failure modes cluster into (i) loss-term imbalance, (ii) spectral bias, (iii) ill-conditioned PDE-residual landscape, (iv) violated causality in time. The literature trend is to fix (iii) with second-order/natural-gradient methods, which reframes PINNs as an optimisation problem rather than an architecture problem.
- KANs' sole robust edge (symbolic discovery with few variables) is an interpretability claim, not an accuracy claim; whether discovered formulas are correct is evaluated case by case.

## Q3. State of SBI methods and their reliability checks

### Takeaway
SBI is mature enough to be used in production science (e.g., gravitational-wave parameter estimation), with NPE/NLE/NRE and flow- or diffusion-based estimators and standard software. The central unresolved issue is trust: posteriors from neural SBI are frequently overconfident or biased, coverage/calibration diagnostics exist (SBC, TARP, L-C2ST, importance-sampling checks) but test only calibration, not correctness, and misspecification (simulator != reality) is the largest open problem.

### Cited Findings

**Methods (all verified ids)**
- Overview: Cranmer, Brehmer, Louppe "The frontier of simulation-based inference" [1911.01429](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01429) (2019, PNAS). Benchmark of the main algorithms: Lueckmann et al. "Benchmarking SBI" [2101.04653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04653) (2021).
- Neural posterior estimation (NPE): train a conditional density estimator q(theta|x) on (theta, x) pairs from prior x simulator, amortised across x. Papamakarios & Murray SNPE-A [1605.06376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06376) (2016); Lueckmann et al. SNPE-B [1711.01861](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01861) (2017); Greenberg et al. APT/SNPE-C [1905.07488](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07488) (2019) (sequential rounds without importance weights). Modern forms use normalizing flows, flow matching (Dax et al. [2305.17161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17161), 2023), or diffusion/score models: Sharrock et al. NPSE [2210.04872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04872) (2022); Geffner et al. compositional score modelling [2209.14249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14249) (2022); Gloeckler et al. Simformer "All-in-one SBI" [2404.09636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09636) (2024).
- Neural likelihood (NLE): learn p(x|theta) then sample with MCMC; Papamakarios et al. SNL [1805.07226](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07226) (2018).
- Neural ratio estimation (NRE): train a classifier to distinguish joint from marginal pairs, whose logit estimates the likelihood-to-evidence ratio; Hermans et al. AALR-MCMC [1903.04057](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04057) (2019); Durkan et al. contrastive NRE [2002.03712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03712) (2020); Delaunoy et al. balanced NRE for conservative posteriors [2208.13624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13624) (2022).
- Software: "sbi reloaded" [2411.17337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17337) (Boelts et al., 2024, verified).
- Scientific deployment: Dax et al. "Real-time gravitational-wave science with NPE" [2106.12594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12594) (2021); Dax et al. neural importance sampling "Rapid and Reliable GW Inference" [2210.05686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05686) (2022), which adds an exact-likelihood importance-sampling step so that posteriors and evidences are corrected and validity is checkable [mechanism from background knowledge, not re-verified]; Wildberger et al. noise-distribution-shift adaptation [2211.08801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08801) (2022). All verified id/title.
- Maturity: NPE/flow-based amortised SBI = production in GW astronomy and growing in cosmology/neuroscience; diffusion/flow-matching SBI = frontier; NRE = established.

**Reliability checks**
- Simulation-based calibration (SBC): Talts et al. [1804.06788](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788) (2018, verified). Idea: if theta* ~ prior, x ~ simulator, and posterior samples are drawn from p(theta|x), then the rank of theta* among the samples is uniform; non-uniform ranks reveal an inaccurate posterior algorithm. Necessary, not sufficient.
- Hermans et al. "A Trust Crisis in SBI? Your Posterior Approximations Can Be Unfaithful" [2110.06581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581) (2021, verified): title states the finding — common neural SBI methods can yield unfaithful (overconfident) posteriors; follow-up remedy BNRE (Delaunoy, above) and "Balancing SBI for Conservative Posteriors" [2304.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10978) (2023, verified title).
- TARP: Lemos et al. "Sampling-Based Accuracy Testing of Posterior Estimators for General Inference" [2302.03026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03026) (2023, verified). L-C2ST (local classifier two-sample test for conditional posterior at a *specific* observation): Linhart et al. [2306.03580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03580) (2023, verified). Mechanisms of these two are from background knowledge, **[not re-verified]**.
- Practical coverage test: empirical coverage (fraction of simulated truths inside credible regions across levels) is the standard cheap diagnostic; it is averaged over the prior, so does not certify conditional (local) coverage.

### Inferences
- Amortised NPE offers a cheap forward pass but no built-in guarantee; the highest-trust workflow is NPE (or NRE) proposal + a downstream correction that exploits an explicit likelihood (importance sampling) when one exists, plus coverage on held-out simulations from a *different* simulator configuration to probe misspecification.
- Coverage on prior-predictive simulations cannot detect misspecification by construction — an independent real-data check is required (posterior predictive checks, misspecification detectors like Schmitt et al.).

## Q4. Open problems and surprising recent results (through 2026)

### Takeaway
The most credible open problems are (1) honest baselines and benchmarking for every method in this note, (2) equivariance vs scale (and whether symmetries can be learned), (3) conservation/stability in learned dynamics beyond toy systems, (4) conditional/local reliability and misspecification in SBI, and (5) generalisation of operator/foundation models off-distribution. Most 2025-2026 items I found are preprints I only partly verified.

### Cited Findings
- Baseline inflation recurs across subfields: graph transformers ([2309.00367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00367)), fluid-PDE ML ([2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07218)), KANs ([2407.16674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16674)).
- Non-conservative forces can look fine on static benchmarks yet fail dynamically ([2412.11569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11569), via search snippet).
- Scaling question: "Does equivariance matter at scale?" [2410.23179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23179) (conclusion unverified); 2026 turbulence/subgrid papers by McConkey et al. [2602.04695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04695), [2607.26850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26850) (titles verified; contents unverified) suggest equivariance may be learned and locality/rotational equivariance matter in closures.
- A surprising PDE-ML result appeared in search: "Neural Emulator Superiority: When ML for PDEs Surpasses its Training Data" 2510.23111 — **[id/title via search snippet only; claim unverified]**.
- SBI misspecification is an active 2025-2026 area (see Q3 entries).
- Oversmoothing/oversquashing: rewiring may trade one problem for the other (Attali survey, via search).

### Inferences
- A methodological lesson for a real-data project: always report matched-compute classical baselines, and treat gains from "structure" (equivariance, physics loss, KAN activations) as hypotheses to test by ablation against an unstructured model with data augmentation.
- For SBI used with real detector noise, misspecification (non-stationary/non-Gaussian noise) is the dominant risk; combine coverage tests with injections into real noise and importance-sampling or ratio-based correction where possible.
