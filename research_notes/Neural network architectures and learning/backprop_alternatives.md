## Q1. Which alternatives scale beyond MNIST/CIFAR, and what is the honest gap to backprop today (2025-2026)?

### Takeaway
Only a few alternatives have reached ImageNet-class tasks, and each does so with a caveat: block-local/greedy losses (ResNet-50 within ~1.1 pt of end-to-end under self-supervision), equilibrium propagation (ImageNet 32x32 and, in a June 2026 preprint, full ImageNet VGG10 at 13.23% vs 12.2% top-5 error), weight-transport-free feedback alignment variants (ImageNet "nearly match" BP), and ES / zeroth-order methods for LLM fine-tuning (memory-limited regimes). Pure Forward-Forward, Hebbian, DFA and target propagation still show a gap that grows with depth and task difficulty; "at the same wall-clock/compute as BP" is almost never shown, and the pattern in the literature is that the gap is smallest where the alternative keeps backprop *inside* a block or uses BP-like structure.

### Cited Findings

#### Forward-Forward (FF) and local contrastive/goodness methods
- Idea: replace the forward+backward pass with two forward passes (real "positive" data, corrupted "negative" data). Each layer is trained to have high "goodness" on positive and low on negative data; no gradient crosses layers. Hinton's goodness is the sum of squared ReLU activities, G = sum_j y_j^2, and p(positive) = sigmoid(G - theta); layer loss is logistic loss on that, with layer normalization between layers so only the activity *orientation* is passed up. Per-layer update: dL/dW_l = (sigma(G-theta) - label) * 2 y_l x_l^T (purely local). — [Hinton 2022, arXiv:2212.13345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13345) (rule read from the full text I extracted)
- Hinton's own numbers (full text): 4x2000 ReLU FF gets 1.36% MNIST test error (BP reaches similar in ~20 epochs vs 60 for FF); 0.64% with 25 jittered shifts per image; on CIFAR-10 with locally-connected non-conv nets, BP test error 37% (2 hidden layers) / 39% (3) vs FF 41% (goodness for every label), 44-46% for one-pass softmax. Hinton frames FF as a candidate for "mortal computation" and for hardware without precise models, and states no claim of beating BP. — [arXiv:2212.13345 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13345)
- Variants: Layer Collaboration FF adds cross-layer coupling because independent layers block information flow — [Lorberbom et al. 2023, arXiv:2305.12393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12393); Predictive FF adds a recurrent generative circuit, lateral competition and predictive coding — [Ororbia & Mali 2023, arXiv:2301.01452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01452); Mono-Forward keeps per-layer locality but replaces the positive/negative objective with plain per-layer cross-entropy, beating vanilla FF and, on an MLP-Mixer on PathMNIST, beating BP while using ~31% of its memory (a small medical-image set) — [Gong, Li, Abdulla, arXiv:2501.09238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09238). The Mono-Forward result implies that locality, not the contrastive goodness objective, may be what is tractable; it is a single-dataset claim by the authors.
- ImageNet: "Adaptive Spatial Goodness Encoding" claims the first FF-style ImageNet training, 51.58% top-1 / 75.23% top-5; I did not find a matched BP baseline on the abstract page, so the gap there is unquantified by me — [Gong, Staszewski, Xu, arXiv:2509.12394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12394).
- Failure-mode theory (very recent): "The Price of Locality" reports a persistent, depth-growing gap; it identifies (1) an optimization floor from concurrent local updates shifting each layer's input distribution (loss shown PL per layer, but drift still leaves an error floor) and (2) rank-collapse of the layer similarity kernel toward rank one with depth — [Wu, Liu, Chen, arXiv:2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33240) (abstract read; submitted 27 Sep 2026, a preprint, not peer reviewed as far as I know).

#### Predictive coding networks (PCNs)
- Idea: each layer holds an activity x_l and predicts the layer below; prediction error e_l = x_l - f(W_l x_{l-1}); network energy F = 1/2 sum_l ||e_l||^2. Inference: clamp input and output label, relax x by dx_l/dt = -e_l + W_{l+1}^T (e_{l+1} * f'(.)). Learning (local, Hebbian-like): dW_l = eta * (e_l * f'(.)) x_{l-1}^T, applied at the equilibrium. Cost: many (T~10-100) relaxation iterations per example.
- Tutorial/survey: [van Zwol, Jefferson, van den Broek, arXiv:2407.04117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04117). Reviews of brain-BP theories: [Whittington & Bogacz 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2018.12.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2018.12.005); [Lillicrap et al. 2020, DOI 10.1038/s41583-020-0277-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-020-0277-3).
- Benchmarks: the PCX library paper tests PCNs on larger architectures/datasets than commonly used and claims new SOTA *for PCNs*; the abstract gives no BP comparison — [Pinchetti et al., arXiv:2407.01163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01163).
- Depth: standard PCNs were hard to train deep; mu-PC (Depth-mu-P-style parametrisation) trains residual PCNs up to 128 layers on simple classification, with zero-shot transfer of learning rates across width and depth, while addressing only some of the identified pathologies — [Innocenti, Achour, Buckley 2025, arXiv:2505.13124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13124).
- ImageNet: a June 2026 preprint trains a 10-layer conv PCN (VGG10) on full ImageNet via a centered equilibrium-propagation variant, reaching 13.23% top-5 error vs a 12.2% BP baseline, and claims the first PCN/EP-based result at ImageNet scale (single preprint, not independently replicated by me) — [Kerjan, Høier, Scellier, arXiv:2606.03584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03584).

#### Hebbian and local unsupervised rules (Oja, BCM, competitive/SoftHebb)
- Oja's rule (single linear neuron, y = w.x): dw = eta * y (x - y w). Converges to the top principal component with unit norm. — [Oja 1982, DOI 10.1007/BF00275687](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00275687)
- BCM rule: dw = eta * x * y * (y - theta_M), with a sliding threshold theta_M tracking a running mean of y^2 (stabilizes Hebbian runaway; yields orientation-selective receptive fields). — [Bienenstock, Cooper, Munro 1982, DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.02-01-00032.1982](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.02-01-00032.1982)
- Competing hidden units (Krotov & Hopfield): local, unsupervised, hidden units compete so that only strong/anti-strong ones update; features from it feed a supervised top layer — [arXiv:1806.10181](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10181).
- SoftHebb: soft winner-take-all Hebbian layers, no feedback/target/error signals; hidden layers up to five, plus a trained linear readout. Reported (authors' own): MNIST 99.4%, CIFAR-10 80.3%, STL-10 76.2%, ImageNet 27.3% — that is a large ImageNet gap to supervised BP CNNs (I did not look up the BP reference number; BP ResNets are far higher, ~70%+ [unverified here]) — [Journé, Rodriguez, Guo, Moraitis, arXiv:2209.11883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11883).

#### Feedback alignment (FA), direct FA (DFA), weight-transport fixes
- FA: backward pass uses fixed random matrix B_l instead of W_l^T: delta_l = (B_{l+1} delta_{l+1}) * f'(z_l); dW_l = -eta delta_l a_{l-1}^T. Forward weights learn to align with B. — [Lillicrap et al. 2016, Nat Commun, DOI 10.1038/ncomms13276](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13276); preprint [arXiv:1411.0247](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.0247).
- DFA: project the *output* error e directly to every hidden layer through fixed random B_l: delta_l = (B_l e) * f'(z_l); layers update in parallel after one forward pass — [Nøkland 2016, arXiv:1609.01596](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01596).
- DFA on modern tasks: works for neural view synthesis, recommender systems, geometric learning and NLP/Transformers, "close to fine-tuned backpropagation" (abstract does not quantify the gap) — [Launay et al. 2020, arXiv:2006.12878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12878). Failure mode: a "bottleneck effect" prevents alignment in narrow layers, hypothesised as why FA methods have not scaled to large convolutional nets — [Launay et al. 2019, arXiv:1906.04554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04554).
- Weight Mirror and a Kolen-Pollack variant let the feedback path *learn* weights; on ImageNet these beat FA and sign-symmetry and nearly match BP (no quantitative values on the abstract page) — [Akrout et al. 2019 (NeurIPS), arXiv:1904.05391](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05391).
- Benchmark of FA family: initialization and optimizer choice matter significantly; BioTorch framework — [Sanfiz & Akrout, arXiv:2108.13446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13446).

#### Target propagation (TP) / difference TP (DTP)
- Idea: instead of propagating gradients, propagate *targets*. Learn approximate inverses g_l ~ f_l^{-1}; set target for layer l-1 as t_{l-1} = a_{l-1} + g_l(t_l) - g_l(a_l) (the "difference" correction of DTP), then each layer does a local regression step toward its target. — [Bengio 2014, arXiv:1407.7906](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7906) (precursor), [Lee et al. 2015, arXiv:1412.7525](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.7525).
- Theory: TP is closely related to Gauss-Newton optimization (so it is *not* BP in disguise); DTP has a fundamental problem when the network is non-invertible; fix = new reconstruction loss plus direct feedback from the output — [Meulemans et al. 2020, arXiv:2006.14331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14331).
- Scalability test: TP and FA variants do well on MNIST but "noticeably worse than BP" on CIFAR and ImageNet, especially with locally connected units — [Bartunov et al. 2018, arXiv:1807.04587](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04587).

#### Equilibrium propagation (EP) and contrastive Hebbian-type methods
- EP: energy-based/recurrent network with state s, energy E(s,theta) and cost C. Phase 1 (free): relax to s*. Phase 2 (nudged): minimize E + beta*C to get s^beta. Update dtheta = (1/beta) * (dE/dtheta(s*) - dE/dtheta(s^beta)) times eta; as beta -> 0 this equals the gradient of C at the fixed point; local, requires symmetric weights and (in the standard version) infinitesimal beta. — [Scellier & Bengio 2016, arXiv:1602.05179](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05179)
- Scaling bottleneck was gradient-estimator bias from one-sided finite nudging; symmetric (+/- beta) nudging gives 11.7% CIFAR-10 test error vs 86% for standard same-sign nudging (13.2% with asymmetric forward/backward weights) — [Laborieux et al. 2021, arXiv:2006.03824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03824).
- Holomorphic EP gives exact gradients with *finite* teaching signals (first Fourier coefficient of oscillations), reports the first EP ImageNet 32x32 benchmark matching a BP-trained network of the same architecture — [Laborieux & Zenke 2022, arXiv:2209.00530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00530). A search snippet states runs took ~5.5 days for EP vs hours for BP [unverified: not read on the paper itself].
- Without weight symmetry: Jacobian-homeostasis loss lets generalized EP reach ImageNet 32x32 with small loss — [Laborieux & Zenke, ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.02214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02214).
- Comparative study of energy-based learning algorithms for analog computing — [Scellier et al., arXiv:2312.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15103) (title/date verified; findings not read). Single-phase contrastive-Hebbian: Dual Propagation uses dyadic neurons, one inference phase, claims BP-equivalent accuracy and runtime including ImageNet32x32 (ICML 2023) — [Høier, Staudt, Zach, arXiv:2302.01228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01228) (resolved via search; claim from search result, abstract page not fetched).

#### Local / greedy layer-wise / block-wise losses
- Idea: attach an auxiliary head and loss to each layer/block; gradients never leave the block (BP inside the block only). Update: dW_l = -eta * grad_{W_l} L_l(h_l(x)). Allows pipelining and big memory saving, no backward locking.
- Local error signals: per-layer predsim/pred losses with CIFAR/ImageNet-scale nets — [Nøkland & Eidnes 2019, arXiv:1901.06656](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.06656) (title verified; numbers not read).
- Greedy layerwise: 1-hidden-layer auxiliary problems give a CNN beating AlexNet on ImageNet; 2-3 hidden-layer problems give an 11-layer net beating several VGG members and VGG-11 matched to end-to-end accuracy — [Belilovsky et al. 2018, arXiv:1812.11446](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.11446).
- Self-supervised blocks: ResNet-50 four blocks each trained with Barlow Twins loss independently; linear-probe ImageNet top-1 70.48% vs 71.57% end-to-end (gap 1.1 pt) — [Siddiqui et al. 2023, arXiv:2302.01647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01647).
- Gradient-isolated learning with contrastive predictive losses — [Löwe et al. 2019, arXiv:1905.11786](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11786) (title verified; results not read).
- Diffusion-style: NoProp trains each block independently to denoise a noisy label embedding (local target, BP only within a block) — no forward/backward chain through the whole net. Full-text results: CIFAR-10 test 79.1-80.5% (NoProp-DT) vs 79.3-79.9% BP; CIFAR-100 45.2-46.1% vs 45.9-47.8% BP; GPU memory on CIFAR-10 0.64 GB vs 1.17 GB; but on Tiny ImageNet (limited test) BP prototype 31.3% vs NoProp 25.7%, and continuous-time variants are weaker — [Li, Teh, Pascanu 2025, arXiv:2503.24322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24322) (numbers from the PDF I extracted).
- Pipelined parallel exact gradients with reversible nets: PETRA, accuracy comparable to BP on CIFAR-10, ImageNet32, ImageNet (ResNet-18/34/50) — [Rivaud et al., arXiv:2406.02052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02052) (this is still gradient-based, a parallelism alternative to standard BP scheduling rather than a different learning rule).

#### Synthetic gradients
- Idea: each module predicts the gradient of the loss w.r.t. its output with a small network, S(h) ~ dL/dh; module updates immediately with S(h) and S itself regresses toward the true (or bootstrapped) gradient later. Removes update locking; allows asynchrony. — [Jaderberg et al. 2016, arXiv:1608.05343](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05343); analysis [Czarnecki et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.00522](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00522). Per Launay et al., synthetic-gradient-style methods had not scaled past MNIST/CIFAR-10 — [arXiv:1906.04554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04554).

#### Evolution strategies / neuroevolution
- OpenAI-style ES: sample eps_i ~ N(0,I), evaluate F(theta + sigma eps_i), update theta += (alpha/(n sigma)) sum_i F_i eps_i. Workers share only scalars (common random seeds); scales to >1000 workers; Humanoid in 10 min; competitive on most Atari games after 1 hour — [Salimans et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864).
- Deep neuroevolution: a plain genetic algorithm evolved a DNN of >4M parameters (largest evolved with a traditional EA at the time); Atari ~4 h on one desktop, ~1 h on 720 cores; reportedly faster than ES, A3C, DQN and combines with novelty search for deceptive tasks — [Such et al. 2017, arXiv:1712.06567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06567).
- NEAT evolves topology and weights from minimal structures with speciation — [Stanley & Miikkulainen 2002, DOI 10.1162/106365602320169811](https://doi.org/10.1162/106365602320169811). CMA-ES adapts a full covariance matrix; O(d^2) memory is the standard obstacle at scale — [Hansen tutorial, arXiv:1604.00772](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00772) (the d^2-memory statement is standard knowledge, [unverified here]). Library: [evosax, arXiv:2212.04180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04180).
- Frontier 2025: ES for full-parameter LLM fine-tuning at billion-parameter scale, reported to outperform RL implementations on tolerance to long-horizon/delayed rewards, robustness across base LLMs, less reward hacking, stability (abstract; no numbers on the page) — [Qiu et al., arXiv:2509.24372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24372). EGGROLL imposes rank-r structure on perturbations to raise arithmetic intensity, reports ~100x training speedup for billion-parameter models at large populations (up to 91% of batch-inference throughput), stable pretraining of integer-only recurrent LMs, and competitiveness with GRPO on reasoning post-training — [Sarkar et al., arXiv:2511.16652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16652).

#### Zeroth-order and forward-mode gradient methods
- Forward gradient: sample tangent v ~ N(0,I); g = (grad F . v) v computed with one forward-mode JVP, an unbiased gradient estimate, no backward pass, no stored activations — [Baydin et al. 2022, arXiv:2202.08587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08587). Variance grows with dimension; local losses help a lot — [Ren et al. 2022, arXiv:2210.03310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03310) (perturbing activations, local greedy losses, LocalMixer; matches BP on MNIST/CIFAR-10 and "significantly outperforms" other BP-free methods on ImageNet, but the abstract does not give the BP gap); direction guessed from local losses beats random tangents but it is a heavily qualified result — [Fournier et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.06968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06968).
- MeZO: SPSA with seed-regeneration trick: g = (L(theta + eps z) - L(theta - eps z))/(2 eps) * z, then theta -= lr * g, z regenerated from the seed so no perturbation is stored. Memory equal to inference; up to 12x memory reduction vs BP, a 30B model on one 80 GB A100 (vs 2.7B with BP), up to 2x GPU-hour reduction, experiments up to 66B, "comparable performance on multiple tasks" — [Malladi et al. 2023, arXiv:2305.17333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333) (step counts are much larger than for BP; not stated in abstract, [unverified]).
- Benchmark of many ZO optimizers for LLM fine-tuning across five model families and five fine-tuning schemes; findings: optimizer/task matching matters, forward-gradient method notable, complexity-performance trade-off; proposes block-wise descent, hybrid training, gradient sparsity — [Zhang et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.11592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11592).

#### Physical / analog in-situ learning
- Photonics: in-situ BP using adjoint methods measures exact gradients from light intensities inside the chip (simulation) — [Hughes et al. 2018, arXiv:1805.09943](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09943); first experiment, three-layer 4-port silicon photonic network, >94% test accuracy matching digital simulation, 64-port network simulated on MNIST (via search result; DOI confirmed) — [Pai et al., Science 2023, DOI 10.1126/science.ade8450](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade8450), preprint arXiv:2205.08501.
- Physics-aware training: a hybrid in-situ forward / in-silico backward pass; optical, mechanical, electronic systems doing audio and image classification — [Wright et al., Nature 2022, DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04223-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04223-6), [arXiv:2104.13386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13386).
- BP-free: Physical Local Learning (model-free forward-forward-style), supervised and unsupervised training of deep physical NNs up to 10 layers; digital parameter updates still needed (via search/EPFL news) — [Momeni et al., Science 2023, DOI 10.1126/science.adi8474](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi8474), preprint arXiv:2304.11042 (id surfaced via search; abstract not fetched).
- Self-learning wave systems via Hamiltonian echo backpropagation for any time-reversible Hamiltonian system, shown numerically — [Lopez-Pastor & Marquardt, PRX 13, 031020 (2023), arXiv:2103.04992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04992). Decentralized physical learning: resistor network with local comparison of two identical networks (coupled learning), trains itself without a processor and is damage tolerant — [Dillavou et al., arXiv:2108.00275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00275). EP in hardware context: [Scellier, arXiv:2103.09985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09985) (title verified; content not read).

### Inferences
- Across the methods that *do* approach BP at ImageNet scale (blockwise SSL, greedy layerwise, PETRA, weight-mirror FA, EP with symmetric/near-symmetric weights), each retains either a BP-like inner structure or exact symmetry; methods that discard both (SoftHebb, pure FF, DFA on CNNs, TP) show 40+ point ImageNet gaps or no ImageNet results. This is my synthesis, not a stated claim of any paper.
- The "gap" is not one number: it depends on task (classification vs LLM), whether labels or SSL, whether the architecture was designed for the alternative (LocalMixer, mu-PC residual nets, Barlow Twins blocks), and whether compute-matched comparisons exist (EP ~5.5 days vs hours for BP per a search snippet).
- NoProp on CIFAR-10/100 matching BP while using ~45% less memory, yet dropping on Tiny ImageNet (31.3% vs 25.7%), shows the pattern "parity at CIFAR, gap re-emerges at the next scale up" that recurs in this field.

### Gaps
- No matched-compute ImageNet-scale comparison of FF vs BP found (ASGE reports 51.58% top-1 with no BP baseline on the abstract page).
- Hinton's FF has not been shown on Transformers/LLMs at any scale in anything I verified.
- Numbers for Nøkland & Eidnes 2019, Löwe 2019, Scellier 2312.15103, Scellier 2103.09985 were not extracted (titles/dates only).
- Mono-Forward, NoProp, SoftHebb, Kerjan et al. (2606.03584), "Price of Locality" (2609.33240) are authors' own reports; no independent replication was found.

## Q2. What theoretical equivalences or approximations to backprop have been proven?

### Takeaway
Proven/derived: (a) PC with a fixed-prediction-assumption and in a specific limit gives BP gradients; PC converges asymptotically to exact BP gradients on arbitrary computation graphs; a zero-divergence inference-learning variant is exactly BP; (b) EP gives exact BPTT/RBP gradients in the infinitesimal-nudge limit (and for any nudge in the holomorphic extension); (c) contrastive Hebbian learning is equivalent to BP in a layered network under a limit (Xie-Seung); (d) forward gradients are unbiased estimators of the gradient; (e) negative: TP is Gauss-Newton-like, not BP; DFA and FA yield only approximate, alignment-dependent updates; PC's equivalence is asymptotic and in practice differs (IL approximates implicit SGD).

### Cited Findings
- Whittington & Bogacz: predictive-coding network with local Hebbian-like plasticity approximates BP under assumptions — [DOI 10.1162/NECO_a_00949](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00949) (2017; Crossref-verified title; I did not re-derive the assumptions).
- PC converges "asymptotically (and in practice rapidly) to exact backprop gradients on arbitrary computation graphs" with local rules; PC versions of CNN, RNN, LSTM perform equivalently to BP on challenging benchmarks (abstract) — [Millidge, Tschantz, Buckley, arXiv:2006.04182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04182); PC "can do exact backpropagation on any NN" (published title; arXiv title "Reverse Differentiation via Predictive Coding") — [Salvatori et al., arXiv:2103.04689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04689).
- Exact BP via zero-divergence inference learning, "first algorithm with local update rules that exactly simulates BP (at least asymptotically)" per the NeurIPS meta-review; autonomous variant needs a more complicated rule and some global signaling; criticised for duplicated weight layers — [Song, Lukasiewicz, Xu, Bogacz, NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/fec87a37cdeec1c6ecf8181c0aa2d3bf-Abstract.html) (no arXiv id verified; cited via NeurIPS page/search).
- Review of the PC-BP relationship plus Torch2PC tool — [Rosenbaum, arXiv:2106.13082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13082) (abstract does not state the equivalence precisely).
- Inference learning (IL) "closely approximates implicit stochastic gradient descent", differing from BP's explicit SGD; faster convergence than BP with small minibatches — [Alonso et al. 2022, arXiv:2206.00164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00164). IL optimization pathologies and fixes — [Alonso, Krichmar, Neftci, arXiv:2305.13562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13562).
- Newer line: PC energy-landscape results beyond equivalence (inferring activity before plasticity gives *learning beyond backprop* — better sample efficiency/robustness claims) — [Song et al., Nature Neuroscience 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41593-023-01514-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01514-1) (title/year verified; not read).
- EP's neural-activity changes during phase 2 match error derivatives of recurrent BP (no side network) — [Scellier & Bengio, arXiv:1711.08416](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08416); discrete-time EP updates match BPTT gradients step-by-step (exact under conditions) in an RNN with static input — [Ernoult et al., arXiv:1905.13633](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13633); holomorphic EP exact for finite nudges — [arXiv:2209.00530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00530).
- Contrastive Hebbian learning vs BP in a layered network — [Xie & Seung 2003, DOI 10.1162/089976603762552988](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976603762552988) (title verified; precise conditions not re-read).
- Forward gradient is an unbiased gradient estimator but high-variance; local losses reduce variance — [arXiv:2202.08587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08587), [arXiv:2210.03310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03310).
- TP ~ Gauss-Newton; DTP issue with non-invertible nets — [arXiv:2006.14331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14331). FA/DFA: no equivalence; performance explained empirically via alignment angles — [arXiv:1906.04554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04554).

### Inferences
- "Equivalence" results are asymptotic (equilibrium, small nudge, or particular initialization) and say nothing about speed. A PC network needing ~T relaxation steps costs ~T x the forward/backward of BP, so exact equivalence trades compute for locality.
- Practical implication: equivalence tells you what the algorithm converges to *if* you run the inner loop to convergence; real benchmarks run it truncated (mu-PC, PCX), where the proven equivalence no longer strictly holds.

### Gaps
- I did not find an arXiv id for Song et al. 2020; cited by NeurIPS proceedings URL.
- The precise assumptions (e.g. fixed prediction assumption, zero-divergence initialization) were not re-derived from the full papers.

## Q3. What are the practical motivations (locality, memory, analog/neuromorphic hardware, parallelism, non-differentiable systems)?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced practical wins are memory (MeZO: inference-level memory, up to 12x less; NoProp ~45% less on CIFAR-10; Mono-Forward ~31% memory on one task), embarrassingly-parallel communication-light training (ES: scalars only, >1000 workers), non-differentiable/reward-only objectives (ES for RL and LLM post-training, neuroevolution), and training physical hardware that cannot be differentiated exactly (physics-aware training, Physical Local Learning, EP/coupled learning in analog circuits). Biological plausibility is the original but least practically-evidenced motivation.

### Cited Findings
- Memory: MeZO same memory as inference, up to 12x reduction, 30B model on one A100-80GB vs 2.7B with BP — [arXiv:2305.17333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333). NoProp GPU memory MNIST 0.49 vs 0.87 GB, CIFAR-10 0.64 vs 1.17 GB, CIFAR-100 1.23 vs 1.73 GB (full text) — [arXiv:2503.24322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24322). Mono-Forward ~31% memory on MLP-Mixer/PathMNIST — [arXiv:2501.09238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09238).
- Parallelism / locking: synthetic gradients remove update/forward locking — [arXiv:1608.05343](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05343); DFA updates layers in parallel from the output error — [arXiv:1609.01596](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01596); PETRA parallelizes via reversible architectures and removes weight stashing — [arXiv:2406.02052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02052); local losses for blockwise training — [arXiv:2302.01647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01647); ES communicates only scalars and scales to >1000 workers — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864); GA 1 hour on 720 cores — [arXiv:1712.06567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06567).
- Hardware efficiency of ES at GPU scale: naive ES has low arithmetic intensity; EGGROLL's low-rank perturbations raise it, with ~100x speedup and up to 91% of batch-inference throughput; integer-only recurrent LM pretraining — [arXiv:2511.16652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16652).
- Non-differentiable / reward-only: ES competitive with RL on locomotion/Atari — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864); ES beats RL on long-horizon/delayed reward, reward hacking and stability for LLM fine-tuning — [arXiv:2509.24372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24372).
- Analog/neuromorphic: EP as a route for gradients on physical/neuromorphic hardware (needs symmetric weights, small nudges) — [arXiv:2309.02214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02214); photonic in-situ BP measured from internal light intensity — [arXiv:1805.09943](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09943), experiment [DOI 10.1126/science.ade8450](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade8450); physics-aware training for systems with imperfect digital models — [DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04223-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04223-6); BP-free physical training (some digital updates retained) — [DOI 10.1126/science.adi8474](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi8474); decentralized resistor-network learning with damage tolerance — [arXiv:2108.00275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00275); Hamiltonian echo — [arXiv:2103.04992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04992).
- Hinton's framing for FF: can learn through a "black box" non-differentiable nonlinearity inserted in the forward path; possible low-power analog hardware without precise knowledge of the forward computation — [arXiv:2212.13345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13345) (paraphrase of his stated motivation).
- Biological plausibility: weight transport and non-local credit assignment are the problems local rules are meant to solve — [Lillicrap et al. 2020 review, DOI 10.1038/s41583-020-0277-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-020-0277-3) (review argues backprop-like credit assignment via activity differences is plausible; I did not re-read the full argument).

### Inferences
- The strongest case for abandoning BP is when BP is *unavailable* (non-differentiable reward, a physical device with no clean model, memory-starved fine-tuning), not when it is merely inconvenient.
- Memory savings are partly offset elsewhere: MeZO and ES need many more forward evaluations than BP steps; PC needs many relaxation steps; EP needs two relaxations (5.5 days vs hours per a search snippet). The time-to-accuracy comparison is rarely reported.

### Gaps
- No verified head-to-head wall-clock/energy numbers for analog EP/PC hardware vs GPU BP.
- Hinton's "mortal computation" and black-box-nonlinearity statements are from the FF paper; I did not quote exact wording.

## Q4. Open problems and surprising results

### Takeaway
Open: depth scaling (FF's rank collapse; PC/EP's many-step inference), credit assignment through narrow/convolutional layers (DFA), exact gradients under realistic noise and asymmetry (EP), variance of forward-gradient/zeroth-order estimators with dimension, and evidence at LLM pretraining scale. Surprising: random feedback works at all; "backprop-free" ES is competitive with RL for billion-parameter LLMs; a block-local denoising objective (NoProp) matches BP on CIFAR; a locality ablation (Mono-Forward) suggests the contrastive goodness objective in FF may be unnecessary.

### Cited Findings
- FF failure analysis: depth-growing gap; optimization floor due to input-distribution drift; kernel collapse to rank ~1 — [arXiv:2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33240).
- DFA: narrow-layer alignment bottleneck — [arXiv:1906.04554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04554). TP: invertibility — [arXiv:2006.14331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14331). FA/TP: gap widens on locally connected nets and harder datasets — [arXiv:1807.04587](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04587).
- EP: nudging bias and weight symmetry requirement; time per run — [arXiv:2006.03824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03824), [arXiv:2309.02214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02214).
- PC: depth pathologies only partly fixed by mu-PC; local minima / compute cost of inference — [arXiv:2505.13124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13124), [arXiv:2305.13562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13562).
- Forward gradient: guessing direction in high dimension is the core cost — [arXiv:2306.06968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06968).
- Surprises: random feedback weights support learning (FA) — [arXiv:1411.0247](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.0247); ES competitive with RL — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864); GA competitive with DQN/A3C/ES on Atari with 4M+ parameters — [arXiv:1712.06567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06567); ES on billion-parameter LLMs outperforming RL on several axes (authors' claim) — [arXiv:2509.24372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24372); PC beats BP on sample efficiency in some settings per Song et al. 2024 — [DOI 10.1038/s41593-023-01514-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01514-1); one-hidden-layer greedy training beating AlexNet on ImageNet — [arXiv:1812.11446](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.11446); NoProp memory/perf at CIFAR — [arXiv:2503.24322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24322).

### Inferences
- A consistent open question is whether any alternative closes the gap *at LLM pretraining scale*; the evidence found is only for fine-tuning (MeZO, ES), RL post-training (EGGROLL), and small/medium vision models.
- Two quick candidate experiments for this repo's style of work (not claims): reproduce NoProp's CIFAR-10 parity at fixed compute; test whether a local-loss/ES method can train the CNN-style models in `primordial_blackhole_search` on its injected-signal task, where gradients exist but memory/latency may matter. Both are hypotheses, not findings.

### Gaps
- No verified LLM-pretraining-scale result for FF/PC/EP/DFA/Hebbian rules.
- I could not verify any 2025-2026 benchmark paper giving a single standardized BP-vs-alternatives comparison table; the closest are Bartunov 2018 (older) and Sanfiz & Akrout 2021 (FA only).
- 2025-2026 preprints (2606.03584, 2609.33240, 2509.12394, 2511.16652) post-date most peer review; treat as provisional.
