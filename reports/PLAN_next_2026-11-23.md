# Plan: a consistency-tested coherent "second look" for minutes-long faint chirps

**Project:** DeepStrain (public LIGO data; GitHub `sumit7194/DeepStrain`).
**Status:** written 2026-11-23 for outside review. This is a plan only: nothing here has been built or run.

## 0. Background: what exists today

**The signals.** Mergers of black holes lighter than the Sun ("subsolar"; component masses 0.2–1.0 M☉). No star can
make such a black hole, so one would point to primordial black holes. These signals are faint, and they stay in
LIGO's band for minutes. At leading order, a 0.2+0.2 M☉ binary spends about 351 s above 50 Hz.

**Terms used below.**
- **Matched filter:** correlate the data with a predicted waveform (a *template*). The output is a signal-to-noise
  ratio, *SNR* or ρ.
- **Bank:** a set of templates spanning the possible masses.
- **Coherent:** one correlation over the whole template, keeping its phase.
- **Semi-coherent (n chunks):** cut the template into n pieces, correlate each, and add the pieces' powers, discarding
  their relative phase. This tolerates a coarser bank but loses sensitivity.
- **χ² consistency test:** split a template into p pieces and check that each piece carries its expected share of the
  SNR. A short noise burst ("glitch") puts it all in one piece. *newSNR* is the standard PyCBC statistic that
  down-weights ρ when the χ² is too large.
- **Gating:** zeroing short stretches of very loud data before filtering.
- **Background:** the false-alarm distribution, made by *time slides*: pairing one detector's data with the other's
  from a different time, so no real signal can be coincident.
- **FAR:** false-alarm rate.
- **Sensitive (chirp) distance:** derived from the fraction of simulated signals ("injections", added to real noise)
  that are found at a given FAR.

**The LongChirp benchmark** (our public release, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24399603).
- **Data:** 24 O3a segments of H1 and L1 data, 4,096 s each.
- **Injections:** 1,344 non-spinning TaylorF2 signals with chirp distance uniform on 5–500 Mpc.
- **Background:** 27.3 days from 24 cross-segment slides. It reaches 1/day and 1/week, but not 1/month.

There are three yardsticks:

| Yardstick | What it is | Distance at 1/day / 1/week |
|---|---|---|
| Y0 oracle | optimal network SNR with the true template (not a search) | 155 / 124 / 104 Mpc at SNR ≥ 8 / 10 / 12 |
| **Y1** | 3,235-template equal-mass bank, 0.05% chirp-mass spacing; **last 64 s of each template only**, 8 chunks, newSNR, H1×L1 | **57.5 / 51.7 Mpc** (111 / 101 found) |
| Y2 | a convolutional network on spectrograms + H1×L1 | 48.4 / 46.2 Mpc |

Both realisable searches reach about 1/3 of the oracle.

**The idea under test: a cascade.**
- **Stage 1** = Y1 with a loose threshold of one candidate per minute (network newSNR 10.65). That gives 39,321
  background candidates and catches 16.2% of injections, against 8.3% for Y1 at 1/day.
- **Stage 2** re-examines only those candidates, coherently and over a much longer stretch.

**Test 1 (idealised).** Stage 2 assumed Gaussian noise and the true template. Result: ROOM, up to ~109 Mpc, nearly 2× Y1.
That gain comes from two sources:
- the second look pays a much smaller trials penalty than Y1's search over 27 days with 3,235 templates;
- longer coherent templates collect more SNR.

**Test 2 (real noise, pre-registered).** Stage 2 was a plain coherent SNR on 384-s templates, after PyCBC-default
autogating at 50σ. Result: **NO VERDICT** (two validity gates failed).
- At stage-1 candidate times, the real-noise tail was enormous: survival 10⁻³ at ρ² ≈ 1,254, against 33 for a
  Gaussian twin with the same spectrum.
- Its log-survival slope was −0.0011 per unit ρ², where Gaussian noise gives −0.5.
- Exploratory distance: 7.8 / 6.1 Mpc, about 0.13× Y1.
- **Diagnosis:** sub-threshold transients, too weak to gate, accumulate over minutes-long coherent templates. Y1
  survives the same noise because newSNR carries a χ² test, and test 2's statistic had none.

## 1. Goal and why it matters

**Goal.** Measure whether a stage 2 that is both coherent *and* χ²-tested, with a real local template search (not
assumed true templates), recovers the cascade's headroom on real O3a noise.

**The decision it produces.**
- If it survives, LongChirp gains a fourth yardstick, Y3: "the realisable two-stage ceiling". ML methods for long
  chirps then have a reachable target between Y1 and Y0, and effort belongs in second-look refinement.
- If it dies, we have a measured statement that on real noise, laptop-scale coherent follow-up of minutes-long chirps
  is limited by sub-threshold non-stationarity even with a χ² test. The 1/3-of-oracle gap is then mostly a noise
  property, not a search-design problem.

**What it adds beyond published work.**
- **LVK's O4 subsolar searches** run fully coherent banks with χ² tests at enormous scale: millions of templates, and
  a 25-million-template bank in one independent search. Soni & Nitz (2024) already showed a coarse-to-fine two-stage
  subsolar search. So the method is not new.
- **New:** a public, per-injection measurement on an open benchmark, separating how much of the semi-coherent-bank
  gap a consistency-tested coherent follow-up recovers in real noise. LVK publications do not expose that
  decomposition.
- This is a modest-audience contribution, and it is claimed as such.

## 2. Method

1. **Stage 1, fixed.** The committed Y1 triggers, threshold one per minute. One per hour (655 candidates) is a
   secondary.
2. **Stage-2 statistic.**
   - Per detector, compute the coherent complex SNR z of a template of length L, ending at the merger.
   - Compute the χ² over p equal-expected-SNR time pieces: χ² = p Σᵢ |zᵢ − z/p|², with 2(p − 1) degrees of freedom.
   - Apply newSNR reweighting: ρ̂ = ρ · [(1 + (χ²/dof)³)/2]^(−1/6) when χ²/dof > 1.
   - Network statistic ρ̂²_net = ρ̂_H² + max over |δ| ≤ 10 ms of ρ̂_L².
   - Same whitening and 50σ autogating as test 2.
3. **Real local refiner.**
   - Around each candidate, search a grid in chirp mass (±2 Y1 spacings), mass ratio q ∈ [0.25, 1], and merger
     time (±1 s at full sample rate).
   - Grid spacing is set so that the worst template match is ≥ 0.97 at length L, measured on dev data.
   - The candidate's stage-2 score is its maximum over the whole neighbourhood.
   - **Trials are therefore counted by construction**, not assumed through a parameter, which was the "N_eff"
     idealisation in tests 1 and 2.
4. **Background.**
   - Run the identical refiner at every stage-1 background candidate.
   - The stage-2 threshold at FAR f is the per-candidate maximum exceeded at rate f. The per-candidate probability
     at 1/day is f / rate₁ = 1/1440, which is ~27 events over 27.3 days, so it is **measured directly, not
     extrapolated** (unlike test 2).
   - 1/week rests on ~4 events, which is flagged as noisy.
5. **Foreground.**
   - Use the linearity shortcut validated in test 2 (0.22% agreement): z = ⟨template, injected signal⟩ +
     ⟨template, noise⟩, piece by piece.
   - This avoids building injected strain, which in pycbc retains memory per new waveform length.
6. **Tuning only on the 8 dev segments** (never on test). Tuned there: L ∈ {128, 256, 384} s, p ∈ {4, 8, 16}, and
   the grid spacing. All are frozen before any test number.
   - **Contamination statement:** test-segment noise was already seen through test 2's statistic, and the new
     statistic was designed knowing that failure. The verdict is reported with and without the one probe segment
     looked at in detail.

## 3. Pre-registration sketch

- **Primary:** sensitive chirp distance of the cascade vs Y1 alone at 1/day and 1/week. Paired bootstrap over
  injections, 2,000 resamples, 90% CI.
- **Verdict:**
  - **SURVIVES** if the ratio is ≥ 1.10 at both FARs with the CI lower bound > 1.00;
  - **DIES** if the ratio is < 1.10 at both FARs;
  - **PARTIAL** otherwise, which licenses nothing.

  The result is also reported as a fraction of Y0 and of the idealised 109 Mpc.
- **Gates** (all must pass, or there is no verdict):
  - **K1, known answer.** On the Gaussian twin (PSD-drawn), the χ² of true-template injections has mean
    2(p − 1) ± 5%. The measured threshold matches the Gaussian prediction for the *measured* neighbourhood size
    within ±10% in ρ.
  - **K2, linearity.** The shortcut matches real injected strain within 2% for 5 injections.
  - **F1, must fail: null injections.** Noise only, same times and templates: ≤ 1% pass. This is test 2's G2 without
    its ill-posed KS clause, which compared stage-1-selected times with unselected times, two populations that differ
    by construction.
  - **F2, must fail: glitch decoys.** Loud sine-Gaussian bursts injected at candidate times, at the same plain
    coherent ρ as caught signals: ≤ 5% pass. If χ² does not reject them, it is not doing its job.
  - **P1, positive control, judged against the twin threshold.** ≥ 90% of caught injections with ρ_opt ≥ 15 pass the
    *Gaussian-twin* threshold. The real-noise pass fraction is reported, not gated. Test 2's G4 gated on the real
    threshold and so confused "machinery broken" with "noise is heavy".
- **Meaning of each outcome:**
  - **SURVIVES:** χ² tames the real tail and Y3 is real. It licenses publishing Y3 and building a faster or learned
    refiner.
  - **DIES with gates passing:** the tail is non-stationarity that χ² cannot see. That points at noise modelling, not
    search design.
  - **Gate failure:** report it, and change nothing after the fact.

## 4. Compute (all on the Mac; nothing needs AWS)

| Step | Size | Peak memory | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 kill test (§5) | ~30 min | < 1.5 GB | test 2's probe: 64 templates, 5 s per 2²⁴-sample series on the Mac GPU |
| S1 Y1 on 8 dev segments (to get dev candidates) | ~9.3 h | 2.6 GB | measured: 68–70 min per segment |
| S2 grid spacing + tuning on dev | 2–6 h | < 2 GB | estimate |
| S3 test run (39,321 candidates × 2 detectors × N_tmpl templates, 2²¹-sample snippets, ~10 ms each) | **22–220 h for N_tmpl = 10²–10³** | < 2 GB | estimate; N_tmpl measured in S2 |

- **Pre-declared fallback, decided from cost before any result:** if S2's measured cost puts S3 above 4 days, the
  primary becomes one-per-hour stage 1 (655 candidates, ~60× cheaper), and one-per-minute becomes a declared
  secondary on a subsample.
- **Precision and hardware:** float32 on the Mac GPU (Y1's float32 path agrees with a float64 reference to ~1e-6); no fp64 or special
  libraries.
- **Memory:** every step fits in 16 GB shared, with a process-group memory guard. Jobs run nohup-detached with
  per-segment checkpoints.

## 5. Risks, and the cheapest test that kills the plan

- **Kill test S0 (~30 min).**
  - Run the χ²-reweighted coherent statistic with test 2's fixed 64 templates at the stage-1 candidate times of the
    single probe segment. That segment is already seen, and it is excluded from the verdict's "without" variant.
  - Compare its conditional tail with the twin's.
  - **Stop** if the projected threshold, at a plausible neighbourhood size (10³ trials), puts the projected cascade
    distance below 1.10× Y1, i.e. 63.2 Mpc at 1/day. The projection uses test 2's realised foreground SNRs through
    the same distance estimator. Distance scales as the cube root of a distance-weighted found fraction, not with raw
    counts.
- **Slow power drift is invisible to χ².** Our CNN's false alarms were short-term power drift, not glitches. Drift
  raises every piece together, so χ² cannot flag it. Mitigation candidates are piece-wise PSD normalisation, or a
  short-PSD reweight. Each one adds tuning freedom, so dev only.
- **χ² punishes real loud signals whenever the template mismatches**, so the grid spacing must be fine enough. K1
  and P1 check this.
- **Neighbourhood cost may explode** at L = 384 s, because coherent spacing tightens with length. The fallback above
  handles the cost; it changes the scope, not the rules.
- **1/week rests on ~4 background events.** The verdict needs both FARs, so the noisy one can veto.

## 6. Questions for an outside reviewer

1. Is per-candidate maximum over a real local neighbourhood the right background unit? Does conditioning on stage-1
   selection bias it in either direction?
2. Is Allen-style time-piece χ² plus newSNR the right consistency test for minutes-long signals, or should it be a
   frequency-band χ², a sine-Gaussian χ², or PyCBC's full ranking statistic?
3. Do F2's sine-Gaussian decoys represent the transients that actually killed test 2? Would a "power-drift decoy" be
   the more honest must-fail?
4. Is 1.10× the right bar, and should PARTIAL exist?
5. Does tuning L, p and spacing on dev and then testing on already-seen test noise leave a contamination route we
   have missed?
6. **Is this the best next step at all?** The alternative we weighed is a statistics question on public posteriors:
   whether the GWTC-4 tension with Einstein's theory in ringdown damping times (98.6% credible boundary) is a known
   finite-SNR bias (Kyutoku 2026). It is plausibly higher-impact and cheaper, but it opens a new line, while this
   plan completes an already-published benchmark. We would value the reviewer's view.
