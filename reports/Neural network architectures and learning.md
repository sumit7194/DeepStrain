# Deep learning's zoo runs on shared primitives

*A living landscape of neural-network architectures and learning methods, 2025-2026. Synthesised on 2026-11-20 from the seven note files in `research_notes/Neural network architectures and learning/` (core architectures, unconventional architectures, geometric and scientific networks, self-supervised and generative learning, alternatives to backpropagation, optimization and scaling, adaptation and efficiency). Extend it in place, and keep the verification tags when you do.*

By late 2026 the neural-network landscape looks less like a zoo of rival species and more like a small set of reusable primitives recombined under hardware constraints. Those primitives are a residual stream, a "token mixer" that moves information between positions, a per-token state that gets updated by a learning-like rule, an iteration run to a fixed point or integrated through time, conditional routing, and a training loss that reconstructs, contrasts or denoises. In production, the winners are whatever fits GPUs. That means Transformers with exact, IO-aware attention ([arXiv:2205.14135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135)), sparse mixture-of-experts at trillion-parameter scale ([arXiv:2507.20534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534)), and a growing number of hybrids that keep **about one softmax-attention layer per three to six linear-recurrent layers** ([arXiv:2507.06457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06457)). AdamW remains the default optimizer ([arXiv:2509.02046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046)), diffusion and flow matching dominate generation ([arXiv:2403.03206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206)), and 4-bit weights plus low-rank adapters dominate deployment ([arXiv:2212.09720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09720), [arXiv:2305.14314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314)).

Most "alternative" ideas either dissolved into the mainstream under another name or stayed niche. Fast weights became linear attention and test-time-training layers, neural ODEs became flow matching, and Hopfield networks became a way of reading attention. Capsules faded. Spiking networks, liquid networks, KANs, PINNs and backprop-free learning rules all have real but bounded wins. The most consistent lesson across all seven note files is that **headline advantages shrink under matched baselines**. New optimizers measure about 1.3x at under 0.5B parameters, and that decays to about 1.1x at 1.2B. Graph transformers, KANs, PDE surrogates and long-range state-space benchmarks have all lost much of their reported edge once the baselines got equal tuning.

If you are looking for combinations or a new method, the most promising ground is where the primitives meet. Recurrent state updates could borrow from optimizer theory. The anti-collapse regularizers that self-supervised learning developed could be applied to local learning rules, which fail through a similar collapse. And the same fixed-point and iteration machinery is shared by reasoning models, energy-based learning and diffusion samplers. The hypotheses section near the end lists these as hypotheses, not findings.

## The one-page map: sixty-two methods on one maturity scale

A computer engineer can read the whole field through one analogy. **A neural network is a program whose constants (weights) are found by search instead of written by hand.** "Architecture" is the shape of the program: its loops, its memory and its addressing scheme. "Learning paradigm" is what the search is scored against. "Training method" is the search algorithm itself. "Adaptation and efficiency" covers patching, compressing and running the found program cheaply. The table below puts every family on one maturity scale. Later sections give the evidence for each row.

The tables show a pattern that later sections develop. Everything rated P either maps cleanly onto dense matrix multiplication on GPUs (attention, MoE, CNNs, LoRA, 4-bit kernels) or turns a hard problem into regression (diffusion and flow matching regress a vector field; distillation regresses a teacher). Everything that stayed S or Faded either needs serial iteration that GPUs execute badly (capsule routing, many-step predictive-coding relaxation) or lacks a hardware and software ecosystem (spiking).

## Architecture families are bets on which structure the data has

**Plain language.** Every architecture is a guess about what kind of structure the data has, built into the wiring so the network does not have to learn it. A CNN bets that nearby pixels matter and that a cat is a cat wherever it appears. An RNN bets that the past can be compressed into a fixed-size buffer. Attention bets nothing about order and keeps a growing lookup table of everything it has seen. In CS terms, the families differ mainly in **how their memory grows with input length**. A CNN has a fixed local window. An RNN or SSM has a fixed-size state, like a ring buffer summarised by a hash. Log-linear and test-time-training models have a state that grows slowly. Attention has an append-only log that it scans in full. The core-architecture notes treat almost every 2024-2026 innovation as **a way to interpolate along this memory-growth axis** [Inference].

### MLPs and KANs: the structureless baseline and its challenger

An MLP is stacked affine maps with a pointwise nonlinearity and no built-in structure. Today it survives mainly as the per-token feed-forward block inside Transformers [unverified, standard knowledge]. All-MLP vision models were competitive but not better than ViTs or CNNs at scale. Examples are MLP-Mixer ([2105.01601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01601)), ResMLP ([2105.03404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03404)) and gMLP ([2105.08050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08050)). This motivated the **MetaFormer thesis**: the general block of token mixer, channel MLP, residual and norm matters more than which token mixer you choose ([2111.11418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11418)). MLPs have a documented **spectral bias**: they learn low-frequency functions first ([1806.08734](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08734)). Fourier-feature input mappings fix this for coordinate networks ([2006.10739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10739)).

**KANs** (Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks) move the learnable part from the nodes to the edges. Each edge carries a learnable univariate B-spline, and the nodes simply sum ([2404.19756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19756)). KAN 2.0 adds tooling for discovering symbolic formulas ([2408.10205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10205)). Independent assessments are consistently deflating. At matched parameters and FLOPs, KAN wins only on symbolic-formula representation, and the MLP stays ahead on machine learning, vision, NLP and audio ([2407.16674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16674)); the attribution of that edge to its B-spline activations is [unverified] in detail. FastKAN shows that KANs are radial-basis-function networks under a re-parameterisation ([2405.06721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06721)). A critical assessment argues the link from the theorem to the architecture is weak (arXiv 2407.11075 [id and authors via search snippet only]). A FAIR comparison for differential equations exists ([2406.02917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02917), findings [unverified]). A tabular benchmark finds KANs competitive but costlier (2406.14529 [id via search snippet]), and a small-LM replacement study finds no consistent advantage over parameter-matched MLPs (2607.15525 [id via search snippet; authors/date from search summary only]). Scientific variants include KINN ([2406.11045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11045)) and the Kolmogorov-Arnold Transformer ([2409.10594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10594)). **Maturity: S/F.** The defensible use is small-data, interpretable symbolic science. That is an interpretability claim, not an accuracy claim.

### CNNs: locality and equivariance remain production workhorses

A convolution slides one small filter across the input, so the same detector is reused everywhere. That gives a locality prior, translation equivariance and a compute cost of O(N·k). **ResNet's identity skip connection** ([1512.03385](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385)) made very deep networks trainable, and later families reuse the residual stream almost universally. Efficiency refinements include dilated convolution ([1511.07122](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07122)), depthwise-separable convolutions in Xception and MobileNets ([1610.02357](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02357), [1704.04861](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04861)), EfficientNet compound scaling ([1905.11946](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11946)) and RepVGG re-parameterisation, which trains with multiple branches and runs as a plain convolution ([2101.03697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03697)).

The important 2022-2023 finding is about recipes, not architecture. **ConvNeXt**, a pure ConvNet with Transformer-era recipe choices, competes with Swin ([2201.03545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03545)), and ConvNeXt V2 adds masked-autoencoder co-design ([2301.00808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00808)). **NFNet ConvNets pretrained on JFT-4B match fine-tuned ViTs at comparable compute** ([2310.16764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16764)). The notes conclude that much of the "ViT beats CNN" story was a compute, data and recipe effect. Inductive bias matters most when data are small and washes out with scale. **Maturity: P.** CNNs win on data efficiency, dense prediction and hardware friendliness. They lose on content-dependent long-range mixing.

### RNNs and the recurrent revival: parallel scans rescued the fixed-size state

**Plain language.** A classic RNN reads one token at a time and overwrites a fixed-size memory, like a streaming hash. It is cheap at inference (O(1) per step) but slow to train, because step *t* waits for step *t-1*, and gradients vanish over long ranges. LSTM's gated cell (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997, DOI 10.1162/neco.1997.9.8.1735 [DOI not re-fetched, unverified]) and the lighter GRU ([1406.1078](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.1078)) addressed the gradient problem. Attention itself began as an add-on to RNN encoder-decoders ([1409.0473](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473)).

**Mechanism of the revival.** If the recurrence is *linear* in the hidden state, h_t = a_t ⊙ h_{t-1} + b_t, then composing steps is associative, so a **parallel prefix scan** computes all states in O(log N) depth. The revival exploits exactly this. The LRU uses complex diagonal linear recurrences with stable initialisation and matched deep SSMs on Long Range Arena ([2303.06349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06349)), and a linear recurrence followed by a nonlinear projection is a universal approximator ([2307.11888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11888)). **minGRU/minLSTM** drop the hidden-state dependence from the gates so they can use a scan, and report about 175x training speedups at length 512 [number from abstract, not re-verified in body] ([2410.01201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01201)). **xLSTM** adds exponential gating plus a scalar-memory sLSTM and a fully parallelisable matrix-memory mLSTM ([2405.04517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04517)). Even *nonlinear* RNNs can be parallelised over time with Newton or fixed-point (DEER) solves ([2309.12252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12252)). A second revival recurs in *depth* rather than time, for reasoning: Universal Transformers ([1807.03819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819)), HRM ([2506.21734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734)), Tiny Recursive Models ([2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871)) and Mixture-of-Recursions ([2507.10524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524)). **Maturity: E for LSTM/GRU, F/S for the revival** (reasoning claims contested [unverified]).

### Transformers: exact attention plus kernels beat clever approximations

**Plain language.** Attention is a soft database lookup. Each token emits a query, compares it with every other token's key, and returns a similarity-weighted average of their values. The model can retrieve any past token exactly, so in-context learning and copying come naturally. The cost is compute that is quadratic in length and a key-value cache that grows linearly.

**Mechanism.** softmax(QKᵀ/√d)V is permutation-equivariant, so position must be injected, and training is fully parallel ([1706.03762](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)). The field tried many approximations. These include LSH (Reformer, [2001.04451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04451)), low-rank (Linformer, [2006.04768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04768)), sliding window plus global tokens (Longformer, [2004.05150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150); BigBird, [2007.14062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14062)), random features (Performer, [2009.14794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14794)), segment recurrence (Transformer-XL, [1901.02860](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02860); Block-Recurrent, [2203.07852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07852)) and moving-average gating (MEGA, [2209.10655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10655)), benchmarked on Long Range Arena ([2011.04006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04006)). The notes record that most of these lost in practice to **exact attention made fast by hardware-aware kernels** [unverified, general knowledge]. **FlashAttention** tiles the computation so the N×N matrix never reaches HBM: memory is linear in N, compute is still quadratic ([2205.14135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135)). Later versions are FlashAttention-2 ([2307.08691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08691)) and FlashAttention-3 ([2407.08608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08608)), and Ring Attention shards the sequence across devices ([2310.01889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01889)).

The surviving efficiency path targets the **KV cache**: Multi-Query ([1911.02150](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02150)), Grouped-Query ([2305.13245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13245)) and Multi-head Latent Attention in DeepSeek-V2 ([2405.04434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434)). It also includes **trained sparse attention**, which keeps softmax but reads only selected tokens: Native Sparse Attention ([2502.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11089)) and DeepSeek Sparse Attention in V3.2, added by continued training as the only architectural change from V3.1-Terminus ([2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556)). StreamingLLM showed that a few "attention sink" tokens plus a window support streaming ([2309.17453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)). A sigmoid gate after attention adds nonlinearity and removes sinks ([2505.06708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06708)). The Differential Transformer subtracts two softmax maps to cancel noise ([2410.05258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05258)). Position schemes are RoPE ([2104.09864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864)), ALiBi ([2108.12409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409)), Position Interpolation ([2306.15595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595)) and YaRN ([2309.00071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071)). A surprising result is that **no explicit position encoding (NoPE) can length-generalise better** in decoder-only models ([2305.19466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466)). Full attention still uses its context unevenly: models use the start and end far better than the middle ([2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)). In vision, ViT ([2010.11929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)) needed large data, DeiT made it data-efficient ([2012.12877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12877)), Swin added hierarchy ([2103.14030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)), and ViT scaling was charted in [2106.04560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04560). **Maturity: P.**

### State-space models and linear attention: one object, many update rules

**Plain language.** An SSM is a digital filter whose coefficients are learned. The same linear system can run three ways. It can run as a recurrence, constant memory per step, which suits inference. It can run as one long convolution or a parallel scan, which suits training. Or it can be written in matrix form. Linear attention turns out to be the same object written differently.

**Mechanism.** The continuous system x′ = Ax + Bu, y = Cx + Du is discretised into a linear recurrence. HiPPO supplies an A matrix that provably compresses history onto orthogonal polynomials ([2008.07669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07669)), and LSSL unified the RNN, CNN and continuous-time views ([2110.13985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13985)). S4 used a diagonal-plus-low-rank A for stable training at length 10k+ ([2111.00396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)). It was simplified to diagonal form (DSS, [2203.14343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14343); S4D, [2206.11893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11893)) and to multi-input multi-output with a parallel scan (S5, [2208.04933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04933)). Time-invariant SSMs cannot select by content. H3 added multiplicative interactions to emulate recall ([2212.14052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14052)), and Hyena used implicit long convolutions plus data-controlled gating ([2302.10866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10866)). **Mamba** makes B, C and the step size input-dependent ("selective"), which gives content-based forgetting at linear time through a hardware-aware scan ([2312.00752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)). **Mamba-2** restricts A to a scalar times identity per head. That enables matmul-based chunked algorithms that run 2-8x faster, through the state-space duality ([2405.21060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060)). **Mamba-3** (ICLR 2026) adds exponential-trapezoidal discretisation, complex-valued transitions that solve state-tracking tasks Mamba-2 cannot, and a MIMO form. It reports Mamba-2 perplexity at half the state size, and +0.6 (+1.8 with MIMO) average downstream points over Gated DeltaNet at 1.5B ([2603.15569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15569); figures from search summary of abstract; also [ICLR 2026 page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010352)).

The linear-attention branch reaches the same place from the other side. RWKV is "an RNN at inference, Transformer-like at training" ([2305.13048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13048)). Eagle/Finch add matrix-valued states ([2404.05892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05892)). **RWKV-7** uses a generalised delta rule and recognises all regular languages with constant memory ([2503.14456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14456)). RetNet has explicit parallel, recurrent and chunkwise forms ([2307.08621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08621)). The gated linear attention and delta-rule line runs from GLA ([2312.06635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06635)) through DeltaNet parallelised over length ([2406.06484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484)) to **Gated DeltaNet**. Gated DeltaNet uses gating for fast erasure and the delta rule for targeted overwrite, and beats Mamba-2 and DeltaNet on language modelling, retrieval and long context ([2412.06464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464)). Other members are Based (linear attention plus a sliding window, with an explicit recall-throughput trade-off, [2402.18668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18668)), log-linear attention (state grows logarithmically, [2506.04761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04761)) and Longhorn (SSM as an online learner, [2407.14207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14207)). The "memory as test-time learning" branch includes TTT layers ([2407.04620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620)), Titans ([2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663)) and ATLAS ([2505.23735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23735)), all F/S. Vision SSMs (Vision Mamba, [2401.09417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09417); VMamba, [2401.10166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10166)) are E and not clearly dominant over ConvNeXt, ViT or Swin [unverified].

### The Transformer-versus-recurrence debate ended in hybrids, not a winner

**The failure mode is recall, and it is a capacity law.** A fixed-size state cannot copy arbitrarily long strings. A 2-layer Transformer can copy exponentially long strings, and pretrained Transformers beat pretrained SSMs on copy and retrieval ([2402.01032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032)). Across 17 models, attention-free models trail attention by up to 2.1 perplexity points on the Pile, and **82% of that gap is associative recall** ([2312.04927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927)). Further evidence comes from the 8B NVIDIA study ([2406.07887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07887), hybrid helps; details [unverified]) and in-context-learning probes of Mamba ([2402.04248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04248), details [unverified]).

**State tracking was a second surprise.** Diagonal, non-negative SSMs sit in TC0 like Transformers, so they cannot compose permutations in one pass. The intuition that recurrence is automatically more expressive is an "illusion" ([2404.08819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819)). Transformers' limits come from parallelism ([2207.00729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00729)), and chain of thought raises their expressivity ([2310.07923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07923)). The fix is to **allow negative eigenvalues** in the transition, which lets linear RNNs solve parity ([2411.12537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537)), or complex ones (Mamba-3), or a generalised delta rule (RWKV-7).

**Production converged on hybrids.** Examples are Griffin/Hawk, which matches Llama-2 with more than 6x fewer tokens ([2402.19427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19427)); RecurrentGemma ([2404.07839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07839)); Jamba ([2403.19887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19887)) and Jamba-1.5 ([2408.12570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12570)); Samba ([2406.07522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07522)); Zamba ([2405.16712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16712)); Hymba with parallel heads ([2411.13676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13676)); Nemotron-H ([2504.03624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03624)); Falcon-H1 ([2507.22448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22448)); and MiniMax-01 ([2501.08313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08313)). **Kimi Linear** (Kimi Delta Attention plus MLA, 3B active / 48B total) claims to be the first hybrid that outperforms full attention under fair comparison in short-context, long-context and RL regimes ([2510.26692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692)). Qwen3-Next uses **3 Gated DeltaNet layers per gated full-attention layer**, with about 7x throughput at 4K context and more than 10x beyond 32K versus Qwen3-32B. That is per a secondary source ([Raschka gallery](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/hybrid-attention/)), and Qwen3.5 carries the same ratio ([Labonne, secondary](https://huggingface.co/blog/mlabonne/qwen35)). The official model cards were not fetched. The most independent evidence is a 72-model ablation. **Perplexity is roughly flat across hybrid ratios while recall depends strongly on the ratio**, and it recommends gated-delta or HGRN-2 layers at 3:1 to 6:1 ([2507.06457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06457)). Meta FAIR's study likewise finds hybrids beat homogeneous models on long-context retrieval ([2510.04800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04800), abstract-level; search summary).

The counter-datapoint is **MiniMax-M2, which reverted to full attention**. The team says small-scale hybrid tests looked equal, but linear and sliding-window variants showed reasoning deficits at larger scale, and prefix caching, speculative decoding and numerical precision were less mature for them ([MiniMax post](https://www.minimax.io/news/why-did-m2-end-up-as-a-full-attention-model); [HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/blog/MiniMax-AI/why-did-m2-end-up-as-a-full-attention-model); critique at [Kaitchup](https://kaitchup.substack.com/p/minimax-m2-and-kimi-linear-why-full)). The "severe reasoning deficits" detail came through secondary coverage [unverified], and a sparse-attention MiniMax M3 is likewise [unverified]. The honest summary is that **the question has moved from "which wins" to "how few softmax layers can you keep, and is the serving stack ready"**. Infrastructure maturity can outweigh architectural merit.

### Mixture-of-experts: the one unconventional idea that won outright

**Plain language.** Replace one big feed-forward block with many smaller "expert" blocks and a router that sends each token to only a few. Parameter count (knowledge capacity) grows, and the compute per token barely does. It is a sharded key-value service where each request touches a few shards.

**Evidence.** The modern form is the sparsely gated top-k MoE, with more than 1000x capacity ([1701.06538](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06538)). GShard sharded a 600B model with compiler support ([2006.16668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16668)), and the Switch Transformer used top-1 routing to reach a trillion parameters ([2101.03961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961)). Expert-choice routing inverts the selection so experts pick tokens, with more than 2x faster convergence ([2202.09368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09368)). DeepSeekMoE adds fine-grained and shared experts ([2401.06066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06066)). Granularity scaling laws find common expert-sizing practice suboptimal ([2402.07871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07871)), and auxiliary-loss-free balancing uses a dynamic routing bias ([2408.15664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15664)). Deployed scale includes Mixtral 8x7B with 13B active params matching or beating Llama 2 70B ([2401.04088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04088)), **DeepSeek-V3 at 671B total / 37B active on 14.8T tokens** ([2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437)), the Kimi K2 1T-parameter MoE ([2507.20534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534)), gpt-oss ([2508.10925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10925)), Qwen3 ([2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388); MoE variants [unverified] from the abstract alone) and LFM2's 8.3B/1.5B-active variant ([2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)). **Maturity: P.** The weaknesses are that all experts must be resident in memory, plus load balancing, routing instability and cross-device communication [background]. It won because it is a drop-in FFN replacement that fits existing sharding.

## Structure-aware networks trade flexibility for guaranteed physics

**Plain language.** If you know a law your data obeys exactly, you can wire it in instead of hoping the network learns it. Examples: a molecule's energy does not change when you rotate it; a social graph has no natural node order; a pendulum conserves energy; a fluid satisfies a PDE. The geometric-and-scientific notes identify **four entry points for structure**. You can build it into the *architecture* (weight sharing over a symmetry group). You can build it into the *form of the output* (predict an energy and differentiate it to get forces). You can put it in the *loss* (PDE residuals). Or you can use *data augmentation* (soft symmetry). The Geometric Deep Learning "proto-book" organises grids, groups, graphs, geodesics and gauges under one symmetry blueprint ([2104.13478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13478)). What you gain is data efficiency and correctness by construction. What you lose is flexibility, hardware efficiency and sometimes accuracy at scale.

### Graph neural networks: production message passing, inflated transformer gains

A GNN repeatedly replaces each node's feature with a learned function of itself and a permutation-invariant aggregate of its neighbours. The MPNN formulation h_v′ = U(h_v, AGG M(h_v, h_u, e_uv)) unifies the variants [from background knowledge, not re-verified]. Key papers are GCN ([1609.02907](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907)), GAT ([1710.10903](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10903)) and MPNN for quantum chemistry ([1704.01212](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.01212)). The expressivity ceiling comes from GIN ([1810.00826](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00826)) and "Weisfeiler and Leman Go Neural" ([1810.02244](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02244)): standard message passing is at most as discriminative as the 1-WL test [from background knowledge, not re-verified]. Two depth pathologies are well characterised. **Oversmoothing** means repeated averaging drives features to a graph-determined fixed point ([1801.07606](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07606), [1905.10947](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10947)). **Oversquashing** means exponentially many neighbours get squeezed into a fixed-size vector ([2006.05205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05205); curvature view, [2111.14522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14522); [2306.03589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03589)). The surveys are 2308.15568 and 2411.17429 [id via search snippet], and the rewiring survey warns that rewiring can scale badly and amplify oversmoothing ([Attali et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17429)). Graph transformers (Dwivedi and Bresson, [2012.09699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09699); Graphormer, [2106.05234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05234); GPS, [2205.12454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12454)) add global attention. But **the reported transformer-over-GNN gap on the Long-Range Graph Benchmark largely disappears after basic hyperparameter tuning** ([2309.00367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00367) [id via search snippet]). Tuned GCN, GAT and GraphSAGE match or beat graph transformers on 17 of 18 node-classification sets, with a graph-level extension in 2502.09263 ([search results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09263) [ids via search snippet; Luo et al. node-classification arXiv id not retrieved]). The science success is **GraphCast**, a GNN weather emulator on an icosahedral multimesh ([2212.12794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12794)); its skill versus ECMWF HRES is [from background knowledge, not re-verified]. **Maturity: P for message passing, E for graph transformers, F/contested for rewiring.**

### Equivariant networks: production in chemistry, open question at scale

Group CNNs share weights across rotations and flips ([1602.07576](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07576)). Steerable CNNs make features transform under group representations ([1612.08498](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08498)). Gauge-equivariant CNNs extend this to manifolds ([1902.04615](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04615)). For 3D, tensor field networks combine spherical-harmonic filters with Clebsch-Gordan tensor products ([1802.08219](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08219)). Successors are SE(3)-Transformers ([2006.10503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10503)) and the e3nn library ([2207.09453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09453)). Invariant predecessors are SchNet, which uses distances ([1706.08566](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08566)), and DimeNet, which adds angles ([2003.03123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03123)). EGNN achieves E(n) equivariance cheaply, with only scalar distances and vector updates ([2102.09844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09844)). Interatomic potentials are where it pays: NequIP ([2101.03164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03164); its data-efficiency claim is [from background knowledge, not re-verified]), Allegro ([2204.05249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05249)), MACE ([2206.07697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07697)) and the universal **MACE-MP-0** ([2401.00096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00096)). Whether equivariance still matters at scale is posed directly by "Does equivariance matter at scale?" ([2410.23179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23179)). Its reported answer, that equivariant models are more compute- and data-efficient but scaled non-equivariant transformers partly close the gap, is **[unverified — read before citing the conclusion]**. A 2026 paper suggests equivariance can be *learned* from turbulence data ([2602.04695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04695), claim from title [unverified]; companion [2607.26850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26850), contents unverified).

A concrete structure trade-off is **conservative versus direct forces**. Direct force heads are faster but break energy conservation, which destabilises molecular dynamics. The recommended recipe is to pretrain on direct forces and fine-tune with forces obtained by backprop through the energy ([2412.11569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11569) [id via search snippet]; also eSEN, [2502.12147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12147), claim from title [unverified]). Hamiltonian and Lagrangian networks apply the same "conservation by output form" idea to dynamics. HNN learns H(q,p) and derives motion from Hamilton's equations ([1906.01563](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01563); mechanism [from background knowledge, not re-verified]). Related models are LNN ([2003.04630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04630)), Deep Lagrangian Networks ([1907.04490](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490)), Symplectic ODE-Net ([1909.12077](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12077)), SympNets ([2001.03750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03750)) and the explicit-constraint simplification ([2010.13581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13581)), with a benchmark in [2012.02334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02334) (conclusions [unverified]). They remain **E on clean, low-dimensional toys**. Their known weaknesses (noise sensitivity, no dissipation, non-unique H) are [unverified in this session].

### Neural operators, PINNs and SBI: real wins hedged by weak baselines

**Neural operators** learn a map from one function to another, for example from coefficient field to solution field. One trained network then becomes a fast surrogate across a family of PDE instances ([2108.08481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08481)). FNO multiplies a truncated set of Fourier modes by learned complex weights ([2010.08895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895)). DeepONet takes the inner product of a "branch" net that encodes the input function and a "trunk" net that encodes the query point ([1910.03193](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03193)). Transformer and general-geometry operators include Galerkin Transformer ([2105.14995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14995)), GNOT ([2302.14376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14376)), Transolver ([2402.02366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02366)), UPT ([2402.12365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12365)) and CNO ([2302.01178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01178)). Foundation-model attempts are Poseidon ([2405.19101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19101)) and DPOT ([2403.03542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03542)). Benchmarks are PDEBench ([2210.07182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07182)), PDE-Arena ([2209.15616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15616)) and The Well ([2412.00568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00568)). The sobering independent result is a systematic review: **weak baselines and reporting biases lead to over-optimism in ML for fluid PDEs** ([2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07218); Nature Machine Intelligence, [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/2475825-weak-baselines-reporting-biases-lead-overoptimism-machine-learning-fluid-related-partial-differential-equations)). Its numeric findings were not extracted [unverified]. A claimed surprise, "Neural Emulator Superiority", is 2510.23111 [id/title via search snippet only; claim unverified]. **Maturity: E, F for foundation operators.**

**PINNs** add the PDE residual at collocation points, computed by autodiff, to the loss ([1711.10561](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10561), [1711.10566](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10566)). The failure modes are well documented: loss-term gradient imbalance ([2001.04536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04536)), NTK analysis ([2007.14527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14527)), spectral bias on multiscale problems ([2012.10047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10047)), a hard loss landscape even for simple convection ([2109.01050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01050)), violated causality in time ([2203.07404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07404)) and **ill-conditioning of the residual loss** ([2402.01868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01868)). Fixes include an expert training guide ([2308.08468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08468)), PirateNets ([2402.00326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00326)), function-space natural gradients ([2402.07318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07318)) and Gauss-Newton natural gradients for CFD ([2402.10680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10680)). The trend **reframes PINNs as an optimization problem rather than an architecture problem**. Benchmarks are PINNacle ([2306.08827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08827), findings [unverified]) and "Can PINNs beat the Finite Element Method?" ([2302.04107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04107)). The recollected answer, "generally not for forward solves", is [from background knowledge, not re-verified]. **Maturity: E for inverse problems and data assimilation; not competitive as a general forward solver.**

**Simulation-based inference** is the scientific deployment of generative models. You train a density estimator on (parameter, simulated data) pairs, so that inference on real data becomes one forward pass ([1911.01429](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01429); benchmark [2101.04653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04653)). Its forms are neural posterior estimation (SNPE-A/B/C: [1605.06376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06376), [1711.01861](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01861), [1905.07488](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07488)), with flow-matching ([2305.17161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17161)) and score or diffusion variants (NPSE [2210.04872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04872), compositional scores [2209.14249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14249), Simformer [2404.09636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09636)). The other forms are likelihood estimation (SNL, [1805.07226](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07226)) and ratio estimation, where a classifier's logit is the likelihood-to-evidence ratio ([1903.04057](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04057), [2002.03712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03712), balanced NRE [2208.13624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13624)). The software is sbi ([2411.17337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17337)). It is **production in gravitational-wave astronomy**: DINGO ([2106.12594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12594)), neural importance sampling ([2210.05686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05686); mechanism [from background knowledge, not re-verified]) and noise-shift adaptation ([2211.08801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08801)).

The central weakness is trust. **Neural SBI posteriors can be unfaithful and overconfident** ([2110.06581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581); remedy [2304.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10978)). Calibration diagnostics include SBC ([1804.06788](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788)), TARP ([2302.03026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03026)) and L-C2ST ([2306.03580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03580)); the mechanisms of the last two are [not re-verified]. These diagnostics test calibration, not correctness, and **prior-averaged coverage cannot detect misspecification by construction**. The misspecification literature includes [2209.01845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.01845), [2112.08866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08866), RNPE [2210.06564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06564), robust SNL [2301.13368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13368), learned robust statistics [2305.15871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15871) and RoPE [2405.08719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08719). Recent reviews are 2503.12315, 2512.23748 and 2509.23385 ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12315v1), [source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23748v1)) [ids/summaries via search snippets, not API-verified], plus MA-SBI ([2606.16923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16923), claims [unverified]) and an ICLR 2026 poster ([listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10012116), abstract not read). Watch the terminology: "calibration" means posterior coverage in Talts and Hermans, but a "calibration set" in Wehenkel means real-data correction.

## Unconventional architectures mostly won by dissolving into the mainstream

**Plain language.** This group of ideas (continuous-depth networks, fixed-point layers, associative memories, weight-generating networks, spiking and liquid neurons, cellular automata, capsules, logic hybrids) was meant to replace the standard stack. The unconventional-architecture notes find that, apart from MoE, **none won as itself; several won by being absorbed** under another name. Neural ODEs became flow-matching and SDE diffusion. Fast weights became linear attention and test-time memory. Hopfield networks became an interpretation of attention. Liquid-style gating became hybrid conv/recurrent LMs. Asking whether the original architecture won is therefore the wrong test [Inference].

### Implicit depth: ODEs, DEQs and recurrent reasoners iterate one block

A residual block x + f(x) is one Euler step of dx/dt = f [background]. A **neural ODE** takes the limit, letting a black-box solver define depth, with constant memory and adaptive compute ([1806.07366](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07366)). Augmented ODEs fix the inability of plain ODE flows to cross trajectories ([1904.01681](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01681)). The real niche is irregular time series: Latent ODE/ODE-RNN ([1907.03907](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03907)) and neural CDEs ([2005.08926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08926)), covered comprehensively in [2202.02435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02435). Neural SDEs have memory-efficient adjoints ([2001.01328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01328)) and can be framed as infinite-dimensional GANs ([2102.03657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03657)). The idea came back at scale through score SDEs ([2011.13456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456)) and flow matching ([2210.02747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747)). A **DEQ** skips the iteration count entirely and solves for the fixed point of one weight-tied layer: infinite depth at constant memory ([1909.01377](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377)). Monotone-operator variants guarantee a unique equilibrium ([2006.08591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08591)), and Jacobian regularisation stabilises training ([2106.14342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14342)). No production DEQ was found [unverified/absence]. The live 2025 descendant is **recurrent-depth reasoning**: iterate a block in latent space to scale test-time compute ([2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)), HRM at 27M params ([2506.21734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734)), and TRM at 7M params, which beats HRM on ARC-AGI ([2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871)). Continuous Thought Machines use neuron-level timing and synchronisation as the representation ([2505.05522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05522)). These are small-data, narrow-task results, and independent replication was not checked.

### Associative memory: one Hopfield step equals attention

Dense associative memories use higher-order interactions to interpolate between feature-matching and prototype regimes ([1606.01164](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01164)). Exponential interactions give exponential storage capacity ([1702.01929](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01929)), and there is a biologically plausible two-body form ([2008.06996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06996)). **"Hopfield Networks is All You Need" shows the continuous modern-Hopfield update equals Transformer attention** ([2008.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217)). The Energy Transformer makes attention descend an engineered energy ([2302.07253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07253)). Hopfield-Fenchel-Young networks generalise the energies ([2411.08590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08590)). A NeurIPS 2025 paper adds Hopfield hidden states to self-attention to reduce rank collapse ([2511.20698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20698)). An informal note frames linear attention as an iterated classical Hopfield network ([beren.io blog](https://beren.io/2024-03-03-Linear-Attention-as-Iterated-Hopfield-Networks/), lower authority). The equivalence conditions (specific β, identity projections) were not checked against the full paper. **Maturity: E as theory, F as architecture.** Energy-based sampling costs more, and attention already gets retrieval for free [background].

### Hypernetworks and fast weights became the linear-attention lineage

A hypernetwork generates another network's weights ([1609.09106](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09106)). Fast weights attend to the recent past through rapidly changing weights ([1610.06258](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06258)). The pivotal result is that **linear Transformers are formally equivalent to 1990s fast-weight programmers, and a delta-rule update improves them** ([2102.11174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174)). The descendants are Gated DeltaNet, TTT, Titans and xLSTM, covered above. The 2025 practical hypernetwork is Text-to-LoRA, which generates an adapter from a task description in one forward pass ([2506.06105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06105)).

### Spiking, liquid, cellular, capsule and neuro-symbolic: bounded or faded

**Spiking networks** communicate with binary events in time. That makes them potentially very energy-efficient on event-driven chips, but non-differentiable. Surrogate gradients make backprop possible ([1901.09948](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09948)). Further results are SEW ResNet past 100 layers ([2102.04159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04159)), Spikformer ([2209.15425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15425)), **Spikformer V2 as the first SNN above 80% on ImageNet** ([2401.02020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02020)) and SpikeGPT ([2302.13939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13939)). SpikingBrain-7B and a 76B-A12B hybrid run on MetaX GPUs and report more than 100x time-to-first-token speedup at 4M tokens with 69.15% spiking sparsity ([2509.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276), summary via [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276v3)). SpikingBrain2.0 followed ([2604.22575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22575)). But **these run on GPUs with spike-inspired encodings; efficiency on real neuromorphic silicon is not shown** [Inference]. Energy claims depend on assumptions: one analysis estimates 6-8x efficiency, hardware-independently ([2210.13107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13107)). The hardware is still a research prototype: Intel Hala Point's 1,152 Loihi 2 chips and 1.15B neurons are vendor claims ([Intel](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to-enable-more-sustainable-ai)), and a Nature 2025 review finds challenges at every stack level ([summary](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1071576)). The original Loihi paper is [unverified].

**Liquid time-constant networks** are continuous-time RNNs whose time constants depend on the input ([2006.04439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04439)). CfC gives them a closed form that avoids ODE solvers ([2106.13898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13898)). There is an out-of-distribution drone-navigation demo ([MIT CAP PDF](https://cap.csail.mit.edu/sites/default/files/research-pdfs/Robust%20flight%20navigation%20out%20of%20distribution%20with%20liquid%20neural%20networks.pdf); venue [unverified]). The commercial LFM2 is a conv-plus-attention hybrid found by hardware-in-the-loop search, with no LTC cells claimed, so **"liquid" there names a lineage, not a mechanism** ([2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)). The related LinOSS uses forced harmonic oscillators and beats Mamba/LRU on long sequences ([2410.03943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03943)).

**Neural cellular automata** learn one local update rule and iterate it until a pattern grows and self-repairs ([Distill 2020](https://distill.pub/2020/selforg/), [DOI record](https://mlanthology.org/distill/2020/mordvintsev2020distill-growing)). In 2025 they were applied per task to ARC-AGI as a model-of-computation study ([2506.15746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15746), via search). **Capsules** grouped neurons into pose vectors with iterative routing-by-agreement ([1710.09829](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.09829); stacked capsule autoencoders [1906.06818](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06818)). They **faded**: a 2023 analysis argues none scaled beyond small datasets, the parse-tree idea is absent in practice, and vanishing gradients starve capsules ([2301.01583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01583)). Its 60.07% ImageNet figure is second-hand [unverified], and deep capsule vanishing activations are covered in [2305.11178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11178) (via search snippet). Attention plus augmentation delivered what capsules promised, without iterative routing [Inference].

**Neuro-symbolic** systems pair learning with logic. Differentiable logic work includes dILP ([1711.04574](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04574)), DeepProbLog ([1805.10872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.10872)), Logic Tensor Networks ([2012.13635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13635)), Scallop ([2304.04812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04812)) and differentiable logic-gate networks ([2210.08277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08277)), whose convolutional version reaches 86.29% on CIFAR-10 ([2411.04732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04732)). DreamCoder does wake-sleep library learning ([2006.08381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381)). The strongest results come from **an LLM proposing and a formal checker verifying**. AlphaGeometry solved 25/30 olympiad problems versus 10 for the prior best ([Nature PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06747-5.pdf); [summary](https://research.google/pubs/solving-olympiad-geometry-without-human-demonstrations/)). AlphaGeometry2 reports 84% ([2502.03544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03544) [unverified]). AlphaProof does RL in Lean ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/olympiad-level-formal-mathematical-reasoning-with-reinforcement-learning/), [Nature table](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y/tables/1)). Other examples are Lean Copilot ([2404.12534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12534)) and DeepSeek-Prover-V2 ([2504.21801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21801)). A formal checker supplies a noise-free reward [Inference]. Pure differentiable logic has not scaled beyond benchmark-sized knowledge bases.

## Learning paradigms differ mainly in how they block the trivial answer

**Plain language.** Supervised learning is fitting a labelled lookup table. Self-supervised learning (SSL) learns an encoding from the data's own structure: two views of the same image, a masked patch, the next token. A cheap linear "head" can then do the real task later. Generative modelling learns the data distribution itself, so you can sample from it or use it as a prior. The deep problem in SSL is that **the easiest way to make two views agree is to output a constant**, the same way a hash function that maps everything to zero has no collisions to worry about. Each SSL family is defined by how it prevents that collapse ([Cookbook of SSL, 2304.12210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12210)). The Platonic Representation Hypothesis argues representations across models and modalities converge on a shared statistical model of reality, which would explain why SSL features transfer ([2405.07987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987)).

### Four anti-collapse mechanisms, trending from heuristics to stated targets

**Repulsion (contrastive).** CPC/InfoNCE predicts future latents with a loss that lower-bounds mutual information ([1807.03748](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)); the InfoNCE formula itself is [unverified: formula from background]. SimCLR uses augmentations and big batches as negatives ([2002.05709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)). MoCo uses a momentum-encoder queue ([1911.05722](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05722)). SwAV contrasts cluster assignments ([2006.09882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09882)). CLIP contrasts images with captions ([2103.00020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020)). On theory, the contrastive loss optimises **alignment of positives plus uniformity on the hypersphere** ([2005.10242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10242)), and the spectral contrastive loss has provable downstream guarantees under an augmentation-graph assumption ([2106.04156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04156)). The mutual-information story is incomplete ([1907.13625](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.13625) [unverified: summary of their finding from background; abstract not re-read]; InfoNCE capped at log batch size, [1905.06922](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06922) [unverified: cap statement]). Even contrastive methods can suffer **dimensional collapse** into a low-rank subspace ([2110.09348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09348) [unverified: from title/background]).

**Asymmetry (self-distillation).** BYOL predicts a slow moving-average target and reaches 74.3% ImageNet linear top-1 with no negatives ([2006.07733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07733)). SimSiam needs only stop-gradient ([2011.10566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10566) [unverified: details beyond title]). The only verified theory is a linearised dynamics analysis. It yields DirectPred, which sets the predictor from input statistics and beats a linear predictor by 2.5% ([2102.06810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06810)); its mechanism detail is [unverified]. DINO's centering and sharpening ([2104.14294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14294); mechanism [unverified]) scaled into DINOv2 ([2304.07193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193)) and **DINOv3, which adds Gram anchoring because dense features degrade over long training** ([2508.10104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10104)).

**Explicit statistics (covariance).** Barlow Twins pushes the cross-correlation toward identity ([2103.03230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03230)). VICReg adds variance, invariance and covariance terms ([2105.04906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04906) [unverified: term details]). Contrastive and covariance criteria are algebraically related ([2206.02574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02574)), and **contrastive and non-contrastive methods recover global and local spectral-embedding methods** ([2205.11508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11508)). The folklore divide is mostly an optimisation detail.

**Data-valued targets (masked modelling) and latent prediction (JEPA).** BERT ([1810.04805](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)) and MAE ([2111.06377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06377) [unverified: 75% mask ratio]) cannot collapse, because their target is the data itself. JEPA predicts *representations* of hidden parts instead of pixels: I-JEPA ([2301.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243) [unverified: details]), V-JEPA ([2404.08471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08471)) and **V-JEPA 2**, trained on over 1M hours of video, with 77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2 and robot planning after a little interaction data ([2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985)). A deep-linear analysis suggests JEPA has an implicit bias toward influential, low-noise features ([2407.03475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03475) [unverified: finding summarised from the abstract's first lines only]). **LeJEPA** (Nov 2025) proves the isotropic Gaussian is the optimal embedding distribution for downstream risk. It enforces that distribution with SIGReg, using one hyperparameter at linear cost ([2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544)). That replaces the EMA and stop-gradient heuristics with a stated target. Whether it scales to DINOv3 or V-JEPA 2 level, or has been replicated, is [unverified].

### Generative models: five families, one winning recipe

**Autoregressive** models factor p(x) = ∏ p(x_i|x_<i), which gives exact likelihood but sequential sampling: PixelRNN ([1601.06759](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06759)) and GPT-3 few-shot scaling ([2005.14165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)). Newer variants predict the next scale (VAR, [2404.02905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02905)) or use a per-token diffusion loss instead of a codebook (MAR, [2406.11838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11838)). **VAEs** maximise the ELBO ([1312.6114](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114) [unverified: formula]). VQ-VAE ([1711.00937](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00937)) and VQGAN ([2012.09841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09841)) became tokenizers. **GANs** play generator against discriminator ([1406.2661](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661); WGAN [1701.07875](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07875); StyleGAN [1812.04948](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04948)). They are sharp and fast, but **diffusion beat them on ImageNet in 2021** ([2105.05233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)). **Normalizing flows** are invertible maps with exact likelihood: NICE ([1410.8516](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.8516)), RealNVP ([1605.08803](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08803)), Glow ([1807.03039](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03039)) and continuous FFJORD ([1810.01367](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01367)).

**Diffusion, score matching and flow matching are one family.** Diffusion started from nonequilibrium thermodynamics ([1503.03585](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03585)). NCSN learned the score (gradient of log density) and sampled by Langevin dynamics ([1907.05600](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05600)). DDPM predicts the added noise with a simple weighted-ELBO loss ([2006.11239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239)). DDIM samples deterministically from the same model ([2010.02502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02502)), and Improved DDPM refined schedules ([2102.09672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09672)). **Song et al. unified these as SDEs.** A forward SDE adds noise, the reverse SDE needs the score, and a "probability-flow ODE" shares the same marginals ([2011.13456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456); the VP/VE labelling is [unverified]). There is likelihood training for score models ([2101.09258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09258)) and a Schrödinger-bridge view ([2106.01357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01357)). EDM separated the design space ([2206.00364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00364)). Classifier-free guidance mixes conditional and unconditional scores ([2207.12598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598) [unverified: mechanism summary]). Latent diffusion moved the process into an autoencoder's latent space ([2112.10752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)), and DiT used a Transformer backbone ([2212.09748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)). Tutorials are [2208.11970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11970) and [2209.00796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00796). Theory gives polynomial-time sampling from L2-accurate scores ([2209.11215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11215)).

In 2022, three groups independently reframed generation as **regressing a velocity field** along a path from noise to data. The three are Flow Matching ([2210.02747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747); diffusion paths as a special case [unverified: the special-case claim as phrased from background]), rectified flow along straight lines ([2209.03003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003) [unverified: reflow detail]; one-step InstaFlow [2309.06380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06380)) and stochastic interpolants ([2209.15571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15571)). The unifying guide is [2412.06264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06264), and **Stable Diffusion 3 runs rectified flow in production** ([2403.03206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206)). The notes' synthesis is that for a Gaussian path x_t = a_t x_1 + s_t ε, the score, the noise prediction, the data prediction and the velocity are linear reparameterisations of one another. "Diffusion with an ODE sampler" and "Gaussian-path flow matching" then differ only in loss weighting, schedule and parameterisation [unverified: stated from the Guide/EDM framing, not re-derived here].

**Consistency and flow-map models learn the ODE's integral**, so sampling takes one or a few steps. Examples are Consistency Models ([2303.01469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469) [unverified: mechanism from background; id/title verified]), improved consistency training ([2310.14189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14189)) and ECT ([2406.14548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14548)). sCM unifies diffusion and consistency parameterisations and scales to 1.5B on ImageNet 512 ([2410.11081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11081)). Flow-map matching frames consistency models through interpolants ([2406.07507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07507)). **MeanFlow models the average velocity and reaches FID 3.43 in one step from scratch** ([2505.13447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13447)). The distillation alternatives are progressive ([2202.00512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00512)), guided ([2210.03142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03142)), adversarial ([2311.17042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17042)) and distribution-matching (DMD, [2311.18828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18828); DMD2, [2405.14867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14867)). **Discrete diffusion for text** runs from D3PM ([2107.03006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03006)) through SEDD ([2310.16834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16834)) and MDLM ([2406.07524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524)) to **LLaDA 8B, competitive with LLaMA3 8B** ([2502.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992)). There is no verified frontier head-to-head against AR models.

**2025 simplifications** suggest much published complexity was convention. Noise conditioning is optional, and a noise-unconditional model reaches FID 2.23 on CIFAR-10 ([2502.13129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13129)). **Predicting clean data beats predicting noise or velocity** with tokenizer-free pixel Transformers ([2511.13720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13720)). The VAE can be replaced by pretrained SSL encoders (RAE, [2510.11690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11690)), and aligning DiT features to SSL features speeds training (REPA, [2410.06940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06940) [unverified: summary from title]). Those last two directly bridge representation learning and generation.

### Generative priors for inverse problems: approximations with proven limits

Diffusion models serve as learned priors for posterior sampling. They are used in medical CT/MRI ([2111.08005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08005)), DPS for noisy, nonlinear problems ([2209.14687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14687)), DDRM ([2201.11793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11793)), latent-diffusion solvers with guarantees for linear problems ([2307.00619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00619)), the flow-matching PnP-Flow ([2410.02423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02423)), provable score-prior imaging ([2310.10835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10835)) and astronomical imaging ([2304.11751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11751) [unverified: application detail; title verified]). But **if one-way functions exist, some posterior-sampling instances take superpolynomial time even though unconditional sampling is fast** ([2402.12727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12727)). DPS-style methods are therefore heuristics. Documented reliability hazards are **mode-interpolation hallucination** outside the training support ([2406.09358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09358)), memorisation ([2301.13188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13188) [unverified: finding from title/background]) and **model collapse** when training on generated data ([2305.17493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17493)). The counterpoint is that two networks trained on disjoint large subsets learn nearly the same score ([2310.02557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02557)), and a statistical-physics analysis predicts when reverse diffusion "speciates" and when it "collapses" into memorisation ([2402.18491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18491)). For scientific use, a prior that interpolates between modes can fabricate plausible but non-physical structure. That is why the notes favour amortised NPE with coverage checks over image-quality benchmarks.

## Backprop alternatives close the gap only by keeping backprop's structure

**Plain language.** Backpropagation is reverse-mode automatic differentiation. Every layer must store its activations, wait for an error signal sent back from the output (backward locking), and use the transpose of its forward weights to send it (weight transport). Brains cannot obviously do this, analog hardware cannot do it exactly, and it costs memory. Alternatives replace the backward pass with something local. Examples are a second forward pass, a relaxation to equilibrium, random feedback wires, predicted targets, or plain trial and error (perturb the weights, keep what helps). The honest 2025-2026 verdict from the notes is short. **Methods that approach backprop at ImageNet scale keep either backprop inside a block or exact weight symmetry. Methods that discard both show gaps of 40+ points or have no ImageNet result** [Inference]. This is the note author's synthesis, not a claim made by any single paper.

### The local-rule family and its ImageNet scorecard

**Forward-Forward (FF)** runs two forward passes, on real ("positive") and corrupted ("negative") data. Each layer raises a "goodness" G = Σy_j² on positive data and lowers it on negative data, with p(positive) = σ(G − θ). The update dL/dW_l = (σ(G−θ) − label)·2y_l x_lᵀ is purely local. Hinton reports 1.36% MNIST error and, on CIFAR-10 with locally connected nets, 41% FF error versus 37-39% for backprop. He frames FF as a route to "mortal computation", not as a backprop beater ([2212.13345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13345); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13345)). Variants add cross-layer coupling ([2305.12393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12393)) or predictive circuits ([2301.01452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01452)). **Mono-Forward keeps locality but drops the goodness objective for per-layer cross-entropy, and beats backprop on one small medical set at about 31% of the memory** ([2501.09238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09238)). That suggests locality, not the contrastive objective, is the tractable part. The first FF-style ImageNet result is 51.58% top-1, with no matched backprop baseline on the abstract page ([2509.12394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12394)). A September 2026 preprint, "The Price of Locality", explains the depth-growing gap two ways. There is an **optimisation floor**, because concurrent local updates shift each layer's input distribution. And the layer similarity kernel undergoes **rank collapse toward rank one** ([2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33240)).

**Predictive coding (PC)** has each layer predict the one below and carry an error e_l = x_l − f(W_l x_{l−1}). The network relaxes its activities to minimise F = ½Σ‖e_l‖², then applies the Hebbian-like update ΔW_l ∝ (e_l ⊙ f′) x_{l−1}ᵀ. The cost is 10-100 relaxation steps per example ([tutorial 2407.04117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04117); reviews [DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2018.12.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2018.12.005), [DOI 10.1038/s41583-020-0277-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-020-0277-3)). The PCX library scales PCNs but gives no backprop comparison ([2407.01163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01163)). μ-PC trains 128-layer residual PCNs with learning rates that transfer across width and depth ([2505.13124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13124)). **A June 2026 preprint trains a VGG10 PCN on full ImageNet via centred equilibrium propagation: 13.23% top-5 error versus 12.2% for backprop** ([2606.03584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03584)). It is a single, unreplicated preprint.

**Hebbian rules** are the oldest. Oja's rule Δw = η y(x − yw) finds the top principal component ([DOI 10.1007/BF00275687](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00275687)). BCM adds a sliding threshold ([DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.02-01-00032.1982](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.02-01-00032.1982)). Krotov and Hopfield use competing hidden units ([1806.10181](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10181)). **SoftHebb** reaches 99.4% MNIST, 80.3% CIFAR-10 and only 27.3% ImageNet ([2209.11883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11883)), against backprop ResNets around 70%+ [unverified here].

**Feedback alignment** sends errors backward through *fixed random* matrices B instead of Wᵀ. It works because the forward weights learn to align with B ([1411.0247](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.0247); [DOI 10.1038/ncomms13276](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13276)). DFA broadcasts the output error straight to every layer, so layers update in parallel ([1609.01596](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01596)). It is "close to fine-tuned backprop" on NLP, recommendation and view synthesis ([2006.12878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12878)), but a **narrow-layer bottleneck** blocks alignment in CNNs ([1906.04554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04554)). Learned feedback (Weight Mirror, Kolen-Pollack) **nearly matches backprop on ImageNet** ([1904.05391](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05391)). The BioTorch benchmark shows initialisation and optimiser matter ([2108.13446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13446)).

**Target propagation** sends *targets* backward through learned approximate inverses, using the difference correction t_{l−1} = a_{l−1} + g_l(t_l) − g_l(a_l) ([1407.7906](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7906), [1412.7525](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.7525)). It is **closer to Gauss-Newton than to backprop** and breaks on non-invertible networks ([2006.14331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14331)). TP and FA are "noticeably worse than BP" on CIFAR and ImageNet ([1807.04587](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04587)).

**Equilibrium propagation (EP)** relaxes an energy-based network twice: once free, once "nudged" toward the target. It updates Δθ ∝ (1/β)(∂E/∂θ(s*) − ∂E/∂θ(s^β)), which equals the gradient as β→0 ([1602.05179](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05179)). **Symmetric ±β nudging cut CIFAR-10 error from 86% to 11.7%** ([2006.03824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03824)). Holomorphic EP gives exact gradients with finite nudges and matched backprop on ImageNet 32×32 ([2209.00530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00530)); the "5.5 days vs hours" runtime is [unverified: not read on the paper itself]. A Jacobian-homeostasis loss removes the need for weight symmetry ([2309.02214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02214)). There is a comparative study for analog computing ([2312.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15103)) and single-phase Dual Propagation ([2302.01228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01228); resolved via search, claim from search result).

**Block-local losses** are the most practical branch: attach a loss to each block and backprop only inside it. Examples are local error signals ([1901.06656](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.06656)), greedy layerwise training where a 1-hidden-layer auxiliary net beats AlexNet ([1812.11446](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.11446)), and **ResNet-50 blocks trained independently with Barlow Twins, at 70.48% versus 71.57% end-to-end** ([2302.01647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01647)). Gradient-isolated contrastive learning is [1905.11786](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11786). **NoProp** trains each block to denoise a noisy label embedding. It matches backprop on CIFAR-10 (79.1-80.5% vs 79.3-79.9%) at about half the memory (0.64 vs 1.17 GB), but drops on Tiny ImageNet (25.7% vs 31.3%) ([2503.24322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24322)). PETRA pipelines exact gradients through reversible nets at backprop accuracy on ResNet-18/34/50 ([2406.02052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02052)). It is a scheduling alternative, not a new rule. Synthetic gradients remove update locking by *predicting* the gradient ([1608.05343](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05343); analysis [1703.00522](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00522)), but did not scale past CIFAR-10.

**Perturbation methods** need no gradient at all. OpenAI-style ES updates θ += α/(nσ) Σ F_i ε_i, with workers exchanging only scalars, scaling past 1,000 workers and training Humanoid in 10 minutes ([1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864)). A genetic algorithm evolved a 4M+-parameter network competitive on Atari ([1712.06567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06567)). Related work includes NEAT ([DOI 10.1162/106365602320169811](https://doi.org/10.1162/106365602320169811)), the CMA-ES tutorial ([1604.00772](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00772); its O(d²) memory obstacle is [unverified here]) and evosax ([2212.04180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04180)). In 2025, **ES fine-tuned billion-parameter LLMs**, reportedly beating RL on long-horizon rewards, reward hacking and stability ([2509.24372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24372)). **EGGROLL** uses low-rank perturbations to raise arithmetic intensity. It reports about 100x speedup, up to 91% of batch-inference throughput, integer-only recurrent-LM pretraining, and parity with GRPO on reasoning ([2511.16652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16652)). Zeroth-order methods estimate gradients from forward passes. The forward gradient (v·∇F)v is unbiased but high-variance ([2202.08587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08587)). Local losses tame the variance ([2210.03310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03310)), and guessed directions help only in qualified settings ([2306.06968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06968)). **MeZO** uses SPSA with seed regeneration, so memory equals inference. That fits a 30B model on one 80 GB A100 versus 2.7B with backprop ([2305.17333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333)); its larger step counts are [unverified]. A ZO benchmark for LLM fine-tuning is [2402.11592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11592).

**Physical learning** trains hardware in place. Photonic in-situ backprop measures gradients from light intensities ([1805.09943](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09943)), demonstrated experimentally at >94% ([DOI 10.1126/science.ade8450](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade8450); preprint arXiv:2205.08501). Physics-aware training runs the forward pass in hardware and the backward pass in simulation ([DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04223-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04223-6), [2104.13386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13386)). Physical Local Learning is FF-style training up to 10 layers ([DOI 10.1126/science.adi8474](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi8474); preprint arXiv:2304.11042, id surfaced via search; abstract not fetched). Hamiltonian echo backprop works for time-reversible systems ([2103.04992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04992)), and a resistor network trains itself without a processor ([2108.00275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00275)); EP for hardware is discussed in [2103.09985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09985).

### Proven equivalences are asymptotic and say nothing about cost

Predictive coding approximates backprop under assumptions ([DOI 10.1162/NECO_a_00949](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00949)). It **converges asymptotically to exact backprop gradients on arbitrary computation graphs** ([2006.04182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04182)) and can do exact backprop ([2103.04689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04689); review [2106.13082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13082)). A zero-divergence variant exactly simulates it ([NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/fec87a37cdeec1c6ecf8181c0aa2d3bf-Abstract.html)). Inference learning instead approximates *implicit* SGD ([2206.00164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00164); pathologies [2305.13562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13562)), and PC may learn "beyond backprop" in sample efficiency ([DOI 10.1038/s41593-023-01514-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01514-1); not read). EP's phase-2 dynamics match recurrent-backprop derivatives ([1711.08416](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08416)) and BPTT step by step ([1905.13633](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13633)). Contrastive Hebbian learning equals backprop in a limit ([DOI 10.1162/089976603762552988](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976603762552988)). The catch: **a PC network needing T relaxation steps costs about T times a backprop pass, and benchmarks truncate the inner loop, where the proven equivalence no longer strictly holds** [Inference].

**Where the alternatives genuinely win** is when backprop is *unavailable*, not merely inconvenient. That covers non-differentiable or reward-only objectives (ES), memory-starved fine-tuning (MeZO), and physical devices with no exact model (physics-aware training, EP, coupled learning). Time-to-accuracy is rarely reported, and no alternative has a verified result at **LLM pretraining scale**.

## Training science: AdamW still wins once baselines get equal tuning

**Plain language.** Training is a search over billions of numbers. The optimizer decides each step's direction and size. Normalization keeps signals in a sane numeric range, like automatic gain control. Regularization stops the search from memorising. Parameterization decides how settings must change when the model grows. Scaling laws are fitted curves that predict loss from model size, data and compute. Most of what the field "knows" here is empirical regularity, not theory.

### Optimizers: matrix preconditioning helps modestly, and the edge shrinks with scale

**Adam** divides each weight's momentum step by a running RMS of its gradient ([1412.6980](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6980)). **AdamW** decouples weight decay from that step, which makes the best decay roughly independent of learning rate ([1711.05101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05101)). The newer optimizers mostly precondition *whole matrices*, not individual weights. K-FAC approximates the Fisher matrix as a Kronecker product per layer ([1503.05671](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.05671)). Shampoo keeps one preconditioner per tensor dimension ([1802.09568](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09568)). **SOAP shows Shampoo equals Adafactor run in Shampoo's eigenbasis, and runs Adam in that basis**, reporting over 40% fewer iterations at 360M-660M ([2409.11321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11321)). **Muon** takes the momentum update G = USVᵀ of each 2D hidden matrix and replaces it with UVᵀ. It does this by approximate orthogonalisation with 5 Newton-Schulz iterations (coefficients 3.4445, −4.7750, 2.0315), leaving embeddings, output head and scalars on AdamW ([Jordan blog](https://kellerjordan.github.io/posts/muon/)). The theory frames Adam, Shampoo and Prodigy as **steepest descent under particular norms** ([2409.20325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20325)), with Muon as the spectral-norm member [unverified] in the abstract. Lion keeps only momentum and takes signed steps ([2302.06675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06675)). Sophia uses a clipped diagonal-Hessian estimate ([2305.14342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14342)). **Schedule-Free** removes the learning-rate schedule through iterate averaging ([2405.15682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15682)).

The evidence is split by who ran it. The developers report "Muon is Scalable": about 2x compute efficiency, with the Moonlight 16B-total MoE trained on 5.7T tokens ([2502.16982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16982)). Kimi K2 (1T/32B active, 15.5T tokens) was trained with MuonClip ([2507.20534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534)). Essential AI reports a better compute-time Pareto frontier up to 4B with muP ([2505.02222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02222)). The independent evidence comes from **"Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers"**, which tested ten optimizers at 0.1-1.2B with per-optimizer tuning. It found that tuning AdamW's learning rate alone gave about 2x on a 130M baseline. **Scalar optimizers all land within 1.2x of AdamW. Matrix optimizers give about 1.3x under 520M, falling to about 1.1x at 1.2B, with no downstream gain.** Muon is best at 1-4x Chinchilla data, Soap and Kron at 8x and above. Sophia gave no significant speedup once the data loader was fully randomised ([2509.02046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046)). Another standardised benchmark is [2509.01440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01440) (ranking not read). The AlgoPerf benchmark ([2306.07179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07179)) and competition ([2502.15015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15015)) found **Distributed Shampoo the external-tuning winner and Schedule-Free AdamW the self-tuning winner**. In production, **DeepSeek-V3 was pretrained with AdamW** (β2 = 0.95, decay 0.1) on a warmup-stable-decay-like schedule ([2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437)). WSD itself comes from MiniCPM ([2404.06395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06395)), and Llama 3 is the AdamW baseline family ([2407.21783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)). GLM-4.5's use of Muon is [unverified] ([2508.06471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06471)). **Whether Muon wins at frontier scale is unresolved**: the independent data show a shrinking edge, and the developers show a deployed choice without a matched AdamW run.

### Normalization, regularization and parameterization: boring defaults, live frontier

BatchNorm allows high learning rates and reaches the same accuracy in 14x fewer steps ([1502.03167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03167)). It works by **smoothing the loss landscape, not by fixing covariate shift** ([1805.11604](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11604)). LayerNorm normalises per example ([1607.06450](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06450)), and RMSNorm drops re-centering at 7-64% less run time ([1910.07467](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07467)). **Pre-LN Transformers train without warmup** ([2002.04745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745)). NFNets use adaptive gradient clipping instead of normalization ([2102.06171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06171)). **Dynamic Tanh replaces normalization layers entirely** and matches normalised Transformers across vision and language ([2503.10622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10622)). On regularization: dropout ([1207.0580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0580)), SAM's flat-minima min-max ([2010.01412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01412); its cost [unverified from abstract]), and the finding that **networks fit random labels regardless of explicit regularization** ([1611.03530](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03530)). Label smoothing's attribution to [1512.00567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.00567) is [attribution unverified]. **muP** keeps optimal hyperparameters stable across width, so a 40M proxy can tune a GPT-3 6.7B-class run at about 7% of its cost ([2203.03466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03466)). But transfer also works under other parameterizations, Adam's ε must be scaled, and Adam-atan2 drops ε ([2407.05872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05872)). **CompleteP** adds depth-wise transfer and non-lazy learning, reporting 12-34% compute savings ([2505.01618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01618)).

### Scaling laws: reliable for loss, fragile at the edges

Kaplan found smooth power laws favouring big models trained on modest data ([2001.08361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)). **Chinchilla revised this to scale parameters and tokens equally**: its 70B model, trained on 4x Gopher's data, beat 280B Gopher ([2203.15556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)). The "20 tokens per parameter" rule is not in its abstract [unverified here]. **The Kaplan-Chinchilla disagreement turned out to be a protocol artifact** (last-layer FLOPs, warmup, scale-dependent optimiser tuning) ([2406.19146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19146)). A replication found Chinchilla's third fit inconsistent, with confidence intervals that would need over 600,000 experiments versus fewer than 500 run ([2404.10102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102)). Accounting for inference cost favours smaller models trained longer, with gains continuing to 10,000 tokens per parameter ([2401.00448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00448)). Repeating data is nearly free up to about 4 epochs ([2305.16264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264)). Scaling constants are **optimiser- and tuning-dependent** ([2509.02046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046)). Mapping loss to downstream or RL-era capability is a separate and weaker claim [inference].

### Generalization: phenomena well described, theory still missing

**Double descent** means test error falls again past the interpolation threshold ([1812.11118](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.11118)), over both model size and epochs, with regimes where 4x more data hurts ([1912.02292](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02292)). **Grokking** means a sudden jump to perfect generalisation long after fitting ([2201.02177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02177)). It has a mechanistic account on modular addition: Fourier circuits form gradually, then the memorised solution is cleaned up ([2301.05217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217)). It also has a weight-norm "LU" account that induces or removes grokking at will ([2210.01117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01117)). **Edge of stability** means full-batch GD drives the top Hessian eigenvalue to about 2/η, which conflicts with classical assumptions ([2103.00065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00065)). Landscape visualisation shows that skip connections smooth the landscape ([1712.09913](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09913)). **Lottery tickets** are subnetworks of 10-20% that train in isolation from their original initialisation ([1803.03635](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03635)). The **NTK** is an infinite-width kernel limit ([1806.07572](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07572)), but lazy training arises from a scaling choice and performs worse on hard tasks, so **it is not where useful deep learning happens** ([1812.07956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07956)). What remains mysterious: why SGD solutions generalise, why scaling exponents take the values they do, and how edge of stability extends to preconditioned optimisers like Adam and Muon [inference].

## Adaptation and efficiency: everything moves learning toward inference

**Plain language.** After training, you can adapt the program or make it cheaper. Meta-learning trains a model to be quick to retrain. Continual learning tries to keep learning without forgetting. Test-time training updates weights *during* inference, like JIT compilation for learning. Test-time compute spends more inference FLOPs on thinking or sampling. The compression family exploits a fact about trained networks: they are heavily redundant. Quantization is a lossy codec on weights. Pruning drops rows of a sparse matrix. Distillation trains a small model on a big one's outputs. LoRA is a patch file applied to a frozen binary, and merging is a three-way merge of patches. These analogies are the note author's [Inference].

### Meta-learning, in-context learning and test-time training are one chain

**MAML** trains an initialisation so a few gradient steps solve a new task ([1703.03400](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400)). Prototypical networks classify by distance to class prototypes ([1703.05175](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.05175)). Learned optimisers ([1606.04474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04474)) scaled to **VeLO** ([2211.09760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09760)). FLAN instruction tuning is multitask meta-learning in practice ([2109.01652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)). **In-context learning is learning in the forward pass.** Transformers learn function classes in context ([2208.01066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01066)), and trained self-attention can **implement gradient descent on in-context regression** ([2212.07677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07677)). Test-time adaptation starts with TENT's entropy minimisation ([2006.10726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10726)). At LLM scale, **test-time training gives up to 6x ARC accuracy, 53.0% with an 8B LM (61.9% ensembled with program synthesis)** ([2411.07279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07279)). SEAL lets the model write its own fine-tuning data and rewards useful edits with RL ([2506.10943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10943)). Other entries are perplexity-minimising test-time LoRA (2505.20633 [snippet]) and In-Place TTT, which repurposes MLP projections as fast weights (2604.06169 [snippet]). MAML optimises for "good after a few steps", which is exactly what test-time training does at inference. **TTT is meta-learning without the outer loop, unless trained for it** [background + inference].

### Continual learning: forgetting and plasticity loss are separate failures

**Plasticity loss is severe.** On continual ImageNet, backprop fell from 89% to 77% by task 2,000, about linear-network level, across architectures and optimisers. L2 plus perturbation eases it, and **continual backprop (reinitialising a few little-used units) appears to sustain plasticity indefinitely** ([2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812); Nature 2024 version via [search summary](https://www.amii.ca/research/loss-of-plasticity-in-deep-continual-learning); precursor [2108.06325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06325)). Plasticity loss tracks curvature rather than saturated units ([2303.01486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01486)), and RL agents accumulate **dormant neurons** that ReDo recycles ([2302.12902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12902)). **Forgetting** is the separate failure, where old tasks degrade. EWC slows learning on important weights ([1612.00796](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)). Replay, regularisation and architecture expansion are the standard families ([2404.16789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16789), [2402.01364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01364), both [snippet]). At LLM scale, LoRA still forgets, as a shifted power law that early stopping cannot avoid ([2401.05605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05605)), and **LoRA learns less but forgets less** than full fine-tuning ([2405.09673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)). Nothing solves continual learning in general at LLM scale.

### Test-time compute is a second scaling axis with inverse-scaling traps

**Compute-optimal per-prompt allocation beats best-of-N by more than 4x efficiency and lets a smaller model beat one 14x larger** ([2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)). Repeated sampling raises *coverage*, but only a verifier converts coverage into accuracy ([2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)). Inference scaling laws are charted in [2408.00724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00724). The techniques are chain of thought ([2201.11903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903)), self-consistency ([2203.11171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171)), Tree of Thoughts ([2305.10601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601)), LATS ([2310.04406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04406)) and process reward models ([2305.20050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)). Adaptive computation includes PonderNet ([2107.05407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05407)), Mixture-of-Depths ([2404.02258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02258)) and Quiet-STaR ([2403.09629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09629)). **Pure RL elicits strong reasoning** (DeepSeek-R1, [2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)), and **1,000 SFT examples plus "budget forcing" let s1-32B beat o1-preview by up to 27%** ([2501.19393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393)). The counter-evidence is just as important. **Base models beat RL-trained models at large pass@k, suggesting RL with verifiable rewards sharpens sampling within the base model's range, while distillation can add new patterns** ([2504.13837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837)). Behaviours like verification and backtracking explain who improves under RL ([2503.01307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01307)). And **longer reasoning can lower accuracy**, with five failure modes identified ([2507.14417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14417); diminishing returns 2506.04210, 2604.10739, 2502.18080, all [snippet]). Three different things share the name "test-time compute": sampling plus selection, which needs a verifier; longer serial chains, which need training; and test-time weight updates, the least mature.

### Compression: about 4 bits and about 50-60% sparsity are nearly free, until training gets long

Quantization results: LLM.int8() halves memory with no degradation up to 175B ([2208.07339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07339)). GPTQ reaches 3-4 bits on 175B in about 4 GPU-hours ([2210.17323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17323)). AWQ protects about 1% salient weights ([2306.00978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)). **4-bit is almost universally optimal for total bits versus accuracy** across 19M-176B parameters ([2212.09720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09720)). BitNet trains 1-bit weights ([2310.11453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11453)), and **BitNet b1.58 claims ternary parity with FP16** ([2402.17764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17764); unreplicated). There is a ceiling, though. **Post-training-quantization degradation grows with training data, so extra pretraining can become harmful** ([2411.04330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04330)), and models trained on very large token counts may not tolerate low bits ([2411.17691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17691)). Pruning results: SparseGPT reaches 60% unstructured sparsity on 175B in one shot ([2301.00774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00774)). Wanda prunes by |weight| × activation ([2306.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11695)). Movement pruning plus distillation keeps accuracy at 3% of parameters ([2005.07683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07683)). **Up to half the layers can be removed** from some open models ([2403.17887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17887)). Distillation started with Hinton's soft targets ([1503.02531](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531)), and Minitron's prune-plus-distill gives 8B and 4B models with 40x fewer tokens ([2407.14679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14679)). PEFT results: adapters come within 0.4% of full fine-tuning on GLUE ([1902.00751](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)), LoRA ([2106.09685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)) and QLoRA fine-tune a 65B model on one 48 GB GPU ([2305.14314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314)). Merging results: model soups ([2203.05482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)), task arithmetic ([2212.04089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)) and TIES ([2306.01708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01708)). The notes' inference is that **redundancy is not a constant of the architecture but a measure of how undertrained a model is**. Long training consumes the slack that compression relies on.

## Eight unifying threads reduce the landscape to a few primitives

These threads are the most useful part of the landscape for anyone looking for combinations, because **an idea proven on one side of an equivalence can often be transplanted to the other**. Each thread is marked by how strong the link is: *proven* (a formal equivalence in a cited paper), *algebraic* (an exact rewrite under stated conditions), or *analogy* (a shared structure the notes infer but no single paper proves).

| Thread | Members | Strength of link |
|---|---|---|
| Per-token state update | Linear attention, RNNs, SSMs, fast weights, delta rule, TTT, Titans, ICL-as-GD, MAML | Proven pairwise; full unification [unverified] |
| Associative memory | Modern Hopfield, attention, dense associative memory, Energy Transformer, MoE routing (?) | Proven for Hopfield and attention; MoE link absent |
| Continuous depth | ResNet, neural ODE, CNFs, diffusion SDE, probability-flow ODE, flow matching, SSM discretisation, liquid nets | Algebraic or proven per pair |
| One generative family | Diffusion, score matching, flow matching, stochastic interpolants, consistency/flow maps | Algebraic for Gaussian paths; note author's synthesis [unverified] |
| Fixed points and iteration | DEQ, Hopfield retrieval, EP and PC relaxation, recurrent-depth LMs, HRM/TRM, NCAs, DEER, GNN oversmoothing | Analogy (shared primitive) |
| Steepest descent under a norm | Adam, Shampoo, SOAP, Muon, K-FAC, TP ≈ Gauss-Newton, natural-gradient PINNs | Proven for Adam/Shampoo/Prodigy |
| Contrast of two states as a gradient | EP free vs nudged, contrastive Hebbian, FF positive vs negative, contrastive SSL, coupled learning, SPSA ±ε, ratio estimation | Analogy, with proven limits for EP and CHL |

### Thread 1: a recurrent state updated by a learning rule

**Linear attention is an RNN.** Replace softmax with a kernel feature map, and causal attention becomes S_t = S_{t−1} + φ(k_t)v_tᵀ with an O(1) state ([2006.16236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16236)). **That RNN is a 1991-style fast-weight programmer**: the state is a fast weight matrix written by an outer-product (Hebbian) rule ([2102.11174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174)). Swapping the additive write for the **delta rule** (S_t = S_{t−1} + β(v_t − S_{t−1}k_t)k_tᵀ [background]) is one step of online regression. **TTT generalises this: the state is any model, updated by a gradient step on a self-supervised loss** ([2407.04620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620)). Titans ([2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663)), ATLAS ([2505.23735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23735)) and Longhorn ([2407.14207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14207)) are further points on the same line. The **test-time regression** framework unifies linear attention, SSMs, DeltaNet and softmax attention, the last as a nonparametric regressor ([2501.12352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352)). **State-space duality** connects SSMs and attention through semiseparable matrices ([2405.21060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060)). Attention itself can be written as an RNN ([2405.13956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13956)), or as a "multi-state RNN" with unbounded state that KV eviction compresses ([2401.06104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06104)). On the learning side, a Transformer's forward pass can implement gradient descent ([2212.07677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07677)), and MAML optimises for few-step learning ([1703.03400](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400)). The note authors propose a **design grid**. The axes are state size (fixed, logarithmic, linear), update rule (additive, decayed, delta, gradient step, nonlinear), transition structure (scalar, diagonal, DPLR, dense/orthogonal, negative/complex eigenvalues), readout (linear, softmax, MLP) and hybridisation (interleave, parallel heads, local window). They note that "most published models occupy a few cells" [unverified against exhaustive search]. **Caveat:** these dualities are about the algebra of the sequence mixer. Equal expressiveness class (TC0) does not mean equal learnability or recall, and a single formal equivalence spanning ICL, TTT, fast weights and meta-learning is [unverified] and may not hold.

### Thread 2: attention is one step of associative-memory retrieval

The modern-Hopfield update equals attention ([2008.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217)). Dense associative memory links to feedforward networks ([1606.01164](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01164)) and has exponential capacity ([1702.01929](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01929)). The Energy Transformer makes the link a design principle ([2302.07253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07253)). If attention is *one* retrieval step, then **extra Hopfield iterations are the DEQ-like depth direction**. That inference is not shown in a single paper the notes read. The notes found **no formal result linking MoE routing to the others**, which leaves an open slot (see H9).

### Thread 3: continuous depth links ResNets, neural ODEs, SSMs and diffusion

A residual step is an Euler step [background]. The neural ODE is the continuous limit ([1806.07366](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07366); ResNet [1512.03385](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385)). Continuous normalizing flows (FFJORD, [1810.01367](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01367)) make the ODE a generative model. The score SDE's probability-flow ODE ([2011.13456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456)) and flow matching ([2210.02747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747)) make it trainable without simulation. SSMs are discretised linear ODEs (S4 [2111.00396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396), Mamba [2312.00752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752), LinOSS [2410.03943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03943); the discretised-ODE view is [background]). Mamba-3's exponential-trapezoidal discretisation is literally a choice of ODE integrator ([2603.15569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15569)). Liquid networks and CfC are continuous-time RNNs ([2006.04439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04439), [2106.13898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13898)). **Numerical-integration ideas (step-size control, integrator order, symplecticity) are therefore portable across sequence models, generative samplers and depth.**

### Thread 4: diffusion, score matching and flow matching are reparameterisations

For Gaussian paths, predicting noise, data, score or velocity are linear transforms of each other; this is the note author's synthesis [unverified: stated from the Guide/EDM framing, not re-derived here]. sCM "unifies previous parameterizations of diffusion models and CMs" ([2410.11081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11081)), flow-map matching frames consistency models through interpolants ([2406.07507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07507)), and the Flow Matching Guide is the common reference ([2412.06264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06264)). Consistency, MeanFlow and flow-map models are **amortised integrators** of the same ODE. So the open state-of-the-art questions are about weighting, parameterisation and distillation, not about model class.

### Thread 5: fixed points and iteration are the recurring compute primitive

The same structure appears again and again: run an update until it settles. DEQs solve for it ([1909.01377](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377)). Hopfield networks descend an energy. **EP and PC learn from the settled state**, so their training *is* a fixed-point computation ([1602.05179](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05179), [2006.04182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04182)). Recurrent-depth LMs ([2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)), HRM ([2506.21734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734)), TRM ([2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871)), Universal Transformers ([1807.03819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819)) and PonderNet ([2107.05407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05407)) iterate a block for reasoning. NCAs iterate a local rule ([2506.15746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15746)). DEER *parallelises* a nonlinear RNN by solving its whole trajectory as a fixed point ([2309.12252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12252)). GNN oversmoothing is the *bad* fixed point of repeated averaging ([1905.10947](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10947)). Diffusion sampling is iterative refinement. The tools that make one of these stable or cheap carry over: implicit differentiation, Jacobian regularisation ([2106.14342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14342)), monotone-operator guarantees ([2006.08591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08591)) and consistency-style amortisation.

### Thread 6: optimizers, target propagation and PINN fixes are geometry choices

Adam, Shampoo and Prodigy are steepest descent under different norms ([2409.20325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20325)). SOAP is Adam in Shampoo's eigenbasis ([2409.11321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11321)). K-FAC approximates natural gradient ([1503.05671](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.05671)). Target propagation is Gauss-Newton-like ([2006.14331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14331)). The current PINN fix is natural-gradient or Gauss-Newton optimisation of an ill-conditioned residual ([2402.01868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01868), [2402.10680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10680)). muP and CompleteP are the *parameterisation* side of the same question: which geometry keeps updates well-scaled as the model grows ([2203.03466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03466), [2505.01618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01618)). The delta rule from Thread 1 is a gradient step on a regression loss. **The "update rule" axis of sequence models and the "optimizer" axis of training are therefore the same design space, applied at different timescales.**

### Thread 7: the difference of two states estimates a gradient

EP subtracts free and nudged equilibria ([1602.05179](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05179)). Contrastive Hebbian learning subtracts clamped and free phases ([DOI 10.1162/089976603762552988](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976603762552988)). FF contrasts positive and negative data ([2212.13345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13345)). Coupled learning compares two identical physical networks ([2108.00275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00275)). MeZO differences L(θ+εz) and L(θ−εz) ([2305.17333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333)). Ratio estimation trains a classifier to tell joint from marginal pairs ([2002.03712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03712)). Contrastive SSL pulls positives and pushes negatives ([2002.05709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)). In each case, **a local comparison of two runs replaces global error transport**. That is why this family dominates physical and analog learning.

### Thread 8: collapse is the field's most common failure mode

The notes surface the same pathology in at least eight places, each with a field-specific fix. These are SSL representational and dimensional collapse ([2110.09348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09348)), FF's layer-kernel rank collapse ([2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33240)), GNN oversmoothing ([1801.07606](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07606)), attention sinks ([2309.17453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)) and attention rank collapse ([2511.20698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20698)), DINOv3's dense-feature degradation fixed by Gram anchoring ([2508.10104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10104)), model collapse on generated data ([2305.17493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17493)), dormant neurons ([2302.12902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12902)), and plasticity loss ([2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812)). The fixes are similar in kind. SSL moved from heuristics to an **explicit target distribution** (VICReg, SIGReg in [2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544)). Attention fixed sinks with a gate ([2505.06708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06708)). Continual learning fixes plasticity with injected randomness (continual backprop, ReDo). **No paper the notes found treats these as one phenomenon**, and that gap is a seed (H6).

## Nine recurring lessons, led by shrinking advantages

**Lesson 1: reported advantages shrink under matched baselines.** This is the best-evidenced regularity in the notes, and it holds in every subfield checked. New optimizers fall from claimed 1.4-2x to under 1.2x for scalar methods, and to about 1.1x at 1.2B for matrix methods ([2509.02046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046)). Graph transformers lose their LRGB gap after tuning ([2309.00367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00367) [id via search snippet]). ML for fluid PDEs suffers weak baselines and reporting bias ([2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07218)). KANs lose at matched FLOPs ([2407.16674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16674)). SSM wins on Long Range Arena shrink once Transformers are pretrained on task data ([2310.02980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02980)). ViTs lose their edge over compute-matched ConvNets ([2310.16764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16764)). The Kaplan-Chinchilla split was protocol ([2406.19146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19146)). **Any new-method claim needs an equally tuned baseline, final-checkpoint evaluation and a strong classical comparator before it counts.**

**Lesson 2: ideas win by dissolving into the mainstream under a new name.** Neural ODEs became flow matching, fast weights became linear attention and TTT, Hopfield became the attention lens, and liquid networks became a commercial hybrid lineage. Judging an idea by whether its original form won misses its real impact.

**Lesson 3: hardware fit decides adoption.** MoE won as a drop-in FFN. Exact attention plus FlashAttention beat approximate attention. Every new recurrence waits on a custom kernel (Mamba's scan, Mamba-2's SSD, KDA's DPLR kernel, Mamba-3's MIMO). MiniMax-M2 retreated to full attention partly because its serving stack was immature. Capsules' serial routing and many-step PC relaxation fit GPUs badly.

**Lesson 4: aggregate metrics hide capability differences.** Hybrid perplexity is flat across ratios while recall varies sharply ([2507.06457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06457)). 82% of the attention-free perplexity gap is recall ([2312.04927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927)). Non-conservative force models look fine statically but fail in dynamics ([2412.11569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11569) [id via search snippet]). Prior-averaged coverage cannot see misspecification. **Evaluate with targeted probes** (associative recall, copy, parity and permutation composition, long rollouts, conditional coverage), not only aggregate loss.

**Lesson 5: more is not monotone.** Longer reasoning can lower accuracy ([2507.14417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14417)). More pretraining can hurt post-training quantization ([2411.04330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04330)). More data can hurt in the double-descent regime ([1912.02292](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02292)). RL can narrow coverage ([2504.13837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837)). Continual training can silently drift to linear-network performance ([2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812)).

**Lesson 6: parity at one scale, a gap at the next.** NoProp matches backprop on CIFAR but not Tiny ImageNet ([2503.24322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24322)). FA and TP work on MNIST but not ImageNet ([1807.04587](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04587)). Muon's edge decays with size. Small hybrids look equal but show deficits at larger scale (MiniMax). **Test at least one scale step above where a claim was made.**

**Lesson 7: heuristics get replaced by stated targets, and simplifications keep working.** SSL moved from EMA and stop-gradient tricks to SIGReg's explicit Gaussian ([2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544)). Diffusion lost noise conditioning ([2502.13129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13129)), its VAE ([2510.11690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11690)) and its noise-prediction target ([2511.13720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13720)). Normalization layers became optional ([2503.10622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10622)). Hand-tuned per-scale hyperparameters gave way to principled parameterisations. Much published complexity was historical convention.

**Lesson 8: calibration is not correctness, and equivalence is not efficiency.** SBC, TARP and coverage check calibration, not truth. Posterior sampling is provably hard in general ([2402.12727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12727)). PC and EP equal backprop only asymptotically, and at a multiple of its cost.

**Lesson 9: structure pays where data are scarce and the symmetry is exact, and washes out with scale.** CNN bias helps at small data, and equivariance pays for molecules. The open question is whether scaled unstructured models learn the symmetry anyway ([2410.23179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23179), conclusion [unverified]). A related pattern is that the cleanest wins come from **verifiers**: formal proof checkers (AlphaProof) and checkable answers in test-time search turn a noisy objective into a hard signal.

## Seeds for combinations and novel methods: fourteen hypotheses, not findings

**Everything in this section is a hypothesis.** None of it has been tested, and none of it has been checked against an exhaustive prior-art search. Some of these combinations may already exist in papers the notes did not cover. Before any novelty claim, each seed needs a dedicated literature search, with every citation verified independently. Each seed is built from the threads above and says three things: why it might work, what it would need, and what existing work it would have to beat. Every test should respect the lessons: equal tuning budget for the baseline, final-checkpoint comparison, targeted probes rather than aggregate loss, and at least one scale step above the first positive result.

### H1. Expressive delta-rule layers paired with sparse rather than dense softmax layers

*Why it might work.* The two efficiency paths so far have been explored separately. One keeps softmax but makes it sparse (NSA [2502.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11089), DSA [2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556)). The other replaces most softmax layers with linear recurrences ([2507.06457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06457)). Hybrids keep a few softmax layers only to buy exact recall, and recall is exactly what sparse attention preserves. State tracking is fixed separately by negative or complex eigenvalues ([2411.12537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537), [2603.15569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15569)). The core-architecture notes list this combination as a hypothesis. *What it needs:* kernels for both layer types in one stack, and the serving features MiniMax found missing (prefix caching, speculative decoding). *What to beat:* Kimi Linear ([2510.26692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692)), Qwen3-Next's 3:1 hybrid (secondary sources only), Mamba-3, and pure DSA, at equal compute, with recall and state-tracking probes as well as perplexity.

### H2. Choosing the memory layer's inner optimizer from the steepest-descent family

*Why it might work.* Thread 1 says the delta rule is one plain gradient step on an online regression loss, and TTT generalises it to arbitrary inner updates ([2407.04620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620), [2501.12352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352)). Thread 6 says the training community found matrix-aware steps (Muon's orthogonalisation, SOAP's eigenbasis Adam) beat elementwise steps at small scale ([2509.02046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046)). A well-conditioned write could store more retrievable associations in a fixed-size state. That would push the recall frontier that limits fixed-state models ([2402.01032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032)). *What it needs:* a chunkwise-parallel formulation of the preconditioned update, and Newton-Schulz cost amortised per chunk. Whether ATLAS ([2505.23735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23735)) or a later paper already does this is [unverified] and must be checked first. *What to beat:* Gated DeltaNet ([2412.06464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464)), Titans and ATLAS at equal state size and throughput.

### H3. Plasticity maintenance for memories that learn at test time

*Why it might work.* A TTT or Titans memory is a continual learner running over millions of tokens. Continual learners over long streams lose plasticity, down to linear-network level ([2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812)), and plasticity loss tracks curvature ([2303.01486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01486)). The adaptation notes flag this link as speculative. If a test-time memory saturates, reinitialising little-used memory units (continual backprop, ReDo [2302.12902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12902)) could restore late-context learning. *What it needs:* a benchmark that measures how well a model learns *new* associations late in a very long context, not only retrieval of early ones. *What to beat:* Titans' more-than-2M-token claim ([2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663)), TTT and log-linear attention.

### H4. A state that grows logarithmically and is written by a gated delta rule

*Why it might work.* The memory-growth axis has fixed-state models at one end and attention at the other. Log-linear attention ([2506.04761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04761)) occupies the middle, and the delta rule gives the best known write for fixed states. The core notes list "mixing log-linear state growth with delta-rule" as comparatively unexplored [unverified against exhaustive search]. *What it needs:* a hierarchical chunk structure compatible with DPLR-style kernels. *What to beat:* log-linear attention as published (whether it already includes delta-rule variants is [unverified]), Gated DeltaNet and Titans, on the recall-versus-state-size trade-off that Based made explicit ([2402.18668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18668)).

### H5. Flow-map training to amortise recurrent-depth reasoning

*Why it might work.* Recurrent-depth reasoners spend test-time compute by iterating a block toward a settled answer ([2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171), [2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871)). Generative modelling already solved an analogous cost problem: consistency, flow-map and MeanFlow models learn to jump along an iterative trajectory in one or a few steps ([2303.01469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469), [2406.07507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07507), [2505.13447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13447)). If the reasoning iteration defines a well-behaved trajectory, a self-consistency loss could let the model skip iterations on easy inputs and spend them on hard ones. That matches PonderNet's adaptive-halting goal ([2107.05407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05407)). *What it needs:* trajectories that converge rather than wander. Fixed-point stabilisers from the DEQ literature ([2106.14342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14342)) are the natural precondition. *What to beat:* TRM and HRM at equal inference FLOPs, and Mixture-of-Recursions ([2507.10524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524)). The HRM/TRM results themselves are narrow and contested [unverified], so replicate them first.

### H6. Explicit distributional anti-collapse for local learning rules

*Why it might work.* This is the cleanest cross-field transplant the notes suggest. "The Price of Locality" attributes Forward-Forward's depth-growing gap partly to **rank collapse of the layer similarity kernel** ([2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33240)). SSL faced the same disease and moved to explicit distributional targets that forbid it (VICReg, Barlow Twins, LeJEPA's SIGReg, [2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544)). And the best-performing local method in the notes trains each ResNet block with a Barlow Twins loss and lands within 1.1 points of end-to-end ([2302.01647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01647)). That is consistent with the idea that *the anti-collapse term is what makes locality work*, though it was not designed to test it. Mono-Forward's finding that locality, not the goodness objective, is the tractable part points the same way ([2501.09238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09238)). *What it needs:* per-layer SIGReg or variance-covariance terms added to FF, Mono-Forward and NoProp, then a depth sweep measuring kernel rank and accuracy. The "optimisation floor" from input drift is a separate mechanism this would not fix. *What to beat:* Siddiqui et al., Mono-Forward, NoProp ([2503.24322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24322)) and the FF ImageNet result ([2509.12394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12394)), at matched compute and with an end-to-end backprop baseline.

### H7. Equilibrium propagation for energy-based attention on analog hardware

*Why it might work.* EP needs a network that settles to an energy minimum ([1602.05179](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05179)). Modern Hopfield layers and the Energy Transformer are exactly such networks, with attention as the retrieval step ([2008.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217), [2302.07253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07253)). Holomorphic EP gives exact gradients with finite nudges ([2209.00530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00530)), and Jacobian homeostasis removes the symmetric-weight requirement ([2309.02214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02214)). That could give a locally trainable, transformer-like model for physical substrates, where the backprop-free family has its only clear advantage. *What it needs:* an energy formulation of a full block (attention plus MLP) and a realistic noisy analog simulator. *What to beat:* the VGG10 ImageNet PCN/EP result ([2606.03584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03584)) and a backprop-trained Energy Transformer of equal size, with wall-clock and energy reported, not just accuracy.

### H8. Evolution strategies as the optimizer for verifier-rewarded test-time training

*Why it might work.* Test-time training on ARC uses gradients on augmented examples ([2411.07279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07279)). But the real signal on verifiable tasks is a discrete pass/fail check, and ES handles discrete rewards natively. ES now runs at billion-parameter scale with high GPU utilisation through low-rank perturbations ([2509.24372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24372), [2511.16652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16652)), and it needs only inference memory, as MeZO does ([2305.17333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333)). *What it needs:* an adapter-sized search space and a population budget comparable to best-of-N sampling. *What to beat:* gradient TTT, best-of-N sampling with the same verifier ([2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)) and compute-optimal test-time allocation ([2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)), all at matched inference FLOPs.

### H9. Mixture-of-experts routing as associative-memory retrieval

*Why it might work.* The notes found no formal result linking MoE routing to the Hopfield/attention family, yet a router does key-value lookup over experts. Expert-choice routing already reverses the selection direction ([2202.09368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09368)). Treating router keys as stored patterns could import capacity results ([1702.01929](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01929)) and energy-based load balancing in place of auxiliary losses ([2408.15664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15664)). *What it needs:* a prior-art search first. Memory-layer and product-key approaches likely exist outside these notes [unverified]. *What to beat:* DeepSeekMoE ([2401.06066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06066)) and aux-loss-free balancing at matched active parameters, measured on load balance and on expert-specialisation metrics, which the unconventional-architecture notes list as an open measurement problem.

### H10. A hypernetwork that outputs the adapter test-time training would have found

*Why it might work.* Test-time training spends inference FLOPs on gradient steps ([2411.07279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07279)), while Text-to-LoRA produces an adapter in one forward pass from a task description ([2506.06105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06105)). A MAML-style outer loop ([1703.03400](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400)) could train a hypernetwork to map a task's few examples straight to the adapter TTT would converge to. That amortises TTT the way consistency models amortise ODE sampling. *What it needs:* a corpus of (task, TTT-adapter) pairs and a check that the generated adapter generalises beyond the distribution of training tasks. *What to beat:* gradient TTT, Text-to-LoRA, and plain in-context learning at matched inference cost.

### H11. "Pretrain unconstrained, fine-tune with hard structure" as a general recipe

*Why it might work.* Atomistic ML found that fast direct-force models pretrained at scale, then fine-tuned with conservative (energy-gradient) forces, get the best of both ([2412.11569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11569) [id via search snippet]). The same tension appears elsewhere. Equivariance at scale is an open question ([2410.23179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23179), conclusion [unverified]), symmetry may be learnable ([2602.04695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04695), claim [unverified]), and Hamiltonian networks work only on clean small systems. *What it needs:* a test where an unconstrained pretrained model is fine-tuned into an exactly equivariant or symplectic one. Rollout stability and conservation would be measured, not static error. *What to beat:* MACE-class equivariant potentials ([2206.07697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07697)) and a structured model trained from scratch on the same data.

### H12. Matrix preconditioners for physics-informed training

*Why it might work.* The current diagnosis of PINN failure is **ill-conditioning of the residual loss**, and the current fixes are expensive Gauss-Newton and natural-gradient methods ([2402.01868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01868), [2402.10680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10680)). Shampoo, SOAP and Muon are cheap Kronecker or orthogonalising approximations to exactly this kind of curvature correction ([2409.11321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11321)). *What it needs:* the documented failure cases (convection and reaction, [2109.01050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01050)) as a benchmark, with wall-clock-matched comparison. *What to beat:* Gauss-Newton natural gradient, PirateNets ([2402.00326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00326)) and, per Lesson 1, a classical finite-element solve ([2302.04107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04107)).

### H13. Self-supervised encoders as SBI summary statistics, with trust checks built in

*Why it might work.* Generative modelling is converging on frozen SSL encoders as latent spaces (RAE [2510.11690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11690), REPA [2410.06940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06940)). SBI needs a compact summary of high-dimensional data before the density estimator ([2106.12594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12594)). A masked-modelling encoder pretrained on unlabelled real noise could give summaries that are less tuned to the simulator. That might reduce sensitivity to misspecification, the field's biggest open problem ([2209.01845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.01845)). *What it needs:* coverage tests on held-out simulator configurations plus real-data checks, because prior-averaged coverage cannot detect misspecification ([2112.08866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08866)). *What to beat:* flow-NPE with jointly learned embedding networks, and learned robust statistics ([2305.15871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15871)).

### H14. One scaling law over parameters, tokens, bits and test-time compute

*Why it might work.* Four separate results each bend the Chinchilla picture. Inference cost favours smaller, longer-trained models ([2401.00448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00448)). Long-trained models quantize worse ([2411.04330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04330)). Test-time compute substitutes for size ([2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)). And optimiser choice shifts the constants ([2509.02046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046)). These pull in different directions: "train small for long" makes models harder to compress. A joint law could expose a real optimum that the separate laws hide. *What it needs:* a grid of small models trained, quantized and evaluated with sampling budgets, fitted with the methodological care the Chinchilla replication demanded ([2404.10102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102)). *What to beat:* the individual laws' predictions on held-out configurations.

## Conclusion

The landscape is less a menu of architectures than a small algebra. A memory with some growth rate, written by some update rule, read by some addressing scheme, iterated to some fixed point and trained by some way of comparing two states covers most of what the notes describe, from Gated DeltaNet to equilibrium propagation to MeanFlow. That reframing changes where to look for new methods. The productive moves of 2023-2026 were transplants across an equivalence. Examples are the delta rule moving from fast-weight theory into production LLMs, the probability-flow ODE turning neural ODEs into flow matching, and explicit distributional targets replacing SSL heuristics. The next ones are most likely to come the same way, from carrying an optimizer, an anti-collapse regularizer or an integrator across a boundary the community still treats as separate.

The second takeaway is about evidence, not ideas. The field's most reliable regularity is that early advantages contract under fair comparison and reappear or vanish one scale step later. So the scarce resource is not candidate methods, which the seeds show are plentiful. It is matched-baseline, probe-based, scale-stepped evaluation. A computer engineer entering this space has the most leverage in the benchmark harness, not the architecture diagram. The hypotheses above are worth exactly as much as the baseline they are run against.
